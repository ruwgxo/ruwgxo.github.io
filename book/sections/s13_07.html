Chapter 13 · Section 7 · ~6 pages

Rate-Based Detection: Abuse Patterns Invisible Per Request

Section Overview

One Line Summary

Exfiltration and enumeration campaigns have no per-request signal -- every query is benign and the entire signal is in the aggregate rate and pattern over time; Section 13.07 builds the rate, periodicity, coverage, and distributed-abuse detectors that see this class, and produces the signal the adaptive rate limiter of 13.08 acts on.

Why This Section Exists

Section 13.07 opens the entity family with the attack class that most cleanly demonstrates why behavioural detection exists: abuse that has no per-request signal at all. The Chapter 12 classifiers score each request, and for an exfiltration campaign there is nothing to score -- every individual query is a reasonable, well-formed, benign request. The attacker reconstructing a proprietary dataset issues ten thousand queries that each look exactly like legitimate use, because each one is legitimate use in isolation. The attack exists only in the aggregate: the volume, the rate, the periodicity, and the systematic coverage of an input space. No per-request detector can see it, because the signal is not in any request. This is the purest case of the taxonomy's central claim. Section 13.01 argued that some attacks live entirely in the relationship between requests; rate-based abuse is the canonical example. The entity issuing the queries is behaving abnormally only when its requests are considered together, over time, as a pattern. Detecting it requires aggregating the per-request event stream by entity and measuring properties of the aggregate -- rate, acceleration, periodicity, coverage -- that have no meaning for a single request. The section also handles the adversary's counter-move. Once per-account rate limits exist, the sophisticated attacker distributes the campaign across many accounts, each staying under the per-account limit, so the aggregate attack is reassembled from individually compliant accounts. Detecting distributed abuse requires aggregating not just per account but per credential and per source, looking for the campaign that is invisible at every individual account and visible only when the accounts are considered together -- which begins to point toward the graph family of 13.11.

Position In Chapter

Section 13.07 is the first of the entity-family sections, following the temporal and output- monitoring work and preceding the adaptive rate limiter of 13.08 that enforces against the signal it produces. It consumes the per-request event stream and the per-entity baselines of 13.03, feeds the rate features into the user profile of 13.10, and surfaces the cross-account patterns that the graph detector of 13.11 develops. The relationship to 13.08 is the section's organising handoff: rate-based detection produces the signal, smart rate limiting acts on it -- detection sets the adaptive threshold, enforcement applies it.

Prose Content

Part 1

Title

Abuse With No Per-Request Signal

Body

The defining property of rate-based abuse is that there is nothing to detect in any single request. Consider an attacker reconstructing a proprietary embedding model's behaviour by querying it systematically, or extracting a private knowledge base one reasonable question at a time, or enumerating which inputs a content filter blocks. Each individual query is well-formed, on-topic, and indistinguishable from legitimate use, because in isolation it is legitimate use. The Chapter 12 injection classifier finds no injection; the jailbreak classifier finds no jailbreak; the anomaly detector finds nothing anomalous about a single ordinary query. Every per-request detector passes every query, correctly, because no query is individually an attack. The attack is the aggregate. Ten thousand such queries from one entity over a day, systematically covering an input space, constitute an exfiltration campaign even though no single query does. The signal is in properties that only exist at the aggregate level: the total volume, the rate of requests, how that rate accelerates, how regular the intervals are, and how the queries cover the space of possible inputs. These are not features of a request; they are features of a sequence of requests attributed to an entity. To compute them, the per-request event stream must be aggregated by entity over a time window, and the aggregate must be measured. This is why rate-based detection belongs to the entity family. The unit of detection is the entity over time, not the request and not even the session. The session family of 13.02 caught the multi-turn attack within a conversation; rate-based detection catches the campaign across all of an entity's activity, which may span thousands of sessions. The shift from request to session to entity is a shift in the aggregation unit, and rate-based abuse is the attack that becomes visible precisely at the entity-over-time level and nowhere below it.

Part 2

Title

Rate, Acceleration, and the Burst-Versus-Sustained Distinction

Body

The most basic aggregate feature is the request rate: requests per unit time from an entity, scored against that entity's rate baseline from 13.03. A rate far above the entity's baseline is the first-order signal. But rate alone is incomplete, because the two main rate-abuse signatures differ in their temporal shape, and a detector tuned for one misses the other -- the same burst-versus-ramp distinction from 13.04, now applied to the entity's request volume. Burst abuse is a sudden high-rate flood: a rapid enumeration that fires thousands of requests in minutes. Its signature is high rate and high acceleration -- the rate not only exceeds baseline but climbs steeply. Acceleration, the rate of change of the rate, is the distinguishing feature: a legitimate increase in usage tends to ramp gently, while a burst attack's rate jumps. Monitoring acceleration alongside rate catches the burst at its onset, before the full volume has accumulated. Sustained abuse is the opposite: a moderate rate held steadily for a long time, the patient exfiltration that never spikes but never stops. Its rate may sit only modestly above baseline -- low enough to avoid a rate threshold tuned for bursts -- but sustained for hours or days, it accumulates an enormous total volume. The signature here is not the instantaneous rate but the duration at an elevated rate and the cumulative volume over a long window. A detector that only thresholds instantaneous rate misses sustained abuse because no single moment looks extreme; a detector that also tracks cumulative volume over a long window and time-at-elevated-rate catches it. The rate-based detector therefore monitors rate, acceleration, and cumulative volume together, covering both the burst that spikes and the sustained campaign that grinds.

Part 3

Title

Coverage Patterns: Sweeping an Input Space

Body

Volume and rate measure how much an entity queries; coverage measures what it queries, and coverage reveals a signature that rate alone cannot: systematic enumeration. An attacker mapping a system's behaviour does not query randomly. They sweep an input space methodically -- iterating through a range of inputs, probing each region, covering the space to reconstruct the system's responses across it. This systematic coverage is structurally different from legitimate use, which clusters around the entity's actual needs and revisits a narrow region repeatedly rather than sweeping broadly and uniformly. Coverage-pattern detection measures how an entity's queries are distributed across the input space. A legitimate user's queries cluster: they return to the same topics, the same endpoints, the same regions of input space, because they have a bounded set of real needs. An enumerating attacker's queries spread: they cover the space broadly and often uniformly, touching many regions once rather than a few regions repeatedly, because their goal is coverage rather than use. The breadth and uniformity of coverage is the signal. An entity whose queries suddenly fan out to cover a wide, uniformly-sampled region of input space is exhibiting an enumeration signature, regardless of whether its raw rate is extreme. Coverage detection complements rate detection because the two catch different exfiltration styles. A high-rate broad sweep trips both; but a low-and-slow enumeration that keeps its rate modest to evade rate thresholds still trips coverage detection, because however slowly it proceeds, its queries still fan out systematically across the space. Coverage is the feature that catches the patient enumerator who has solved the rate problem by going slow -- the systematic breadth gives them away even when the volume does not. The coverage signal feeds the same entity rate score and the user profile, adding the what-they-query dimension to the how-much-they-query dimension.

Part 4

Title

Periodicity: The Automation Signature

Body

A distinct and high-value aggregate feature is periodicity: the regularity of the intervals between an entity's requests. Human use is irregular. People pause to read, to think, to do something else, and the intervals between their requests vary widely and unpredictably. Automated use is regular. A script issues requests on a timer or in a tight loop, and the intervals between its requests are strikingly consistent -- the same gap, repeated, because a machine does not pause to think. This regularity is a signature of automation that is hard for an attacker to disguise without sacrificing the throughput that automation provides. Periodicity detection measures the consistency of an entity's inter-request intervals. A low variance in the intervals -- requests arriving like clockwork at a near-constant spacing -- is the automation signature. The measure is the coefficient of variation of the intervals or a spectral/autocorrelation analysis that detects a dominant period: a strong peak at a particular interval means the entity is firing on that period, which humans do not do. The periodicity signal is valuable because it separates automated abuse from heavy-but-human use: a power user who legitimately makes many requests does so irregularly, with the natural variation of human activity, while an exfiltration script makes its requests on a regular cadence. Two entities with the same high request rate can be distinguished by periodicity -- the regular one is automated, the irregular one is plausibly a busy human. Periodicity has a subtlety: a sophisticated attacker can add jitter, randomising the intervals to mimic human irregularity. This raises the attacker's cost -- jitter reduces throughput and complicates the automation -- but it can defeat a naive periodicity detector that looks only for near-constant intervals. The robust response is to look for structure beyond simple regularity: even jittered automation often retains a detectable periodic component, a burst-and-pause rhythm, or a coverage pattern that betrays the underlying script. Periodicity is one signal among several, and it is strongest in combination with the rate, acceleration, and coverage features rather than relied on alone, since each can be individually evaded but evading all of them simultaneously is expensive.

Part 5

Title

Distributed Abuse: The Campaign Across Many Accounts

Body

Per-account rate detection invites an obvious counter-move, and the sophisticated adversary takes it: distribute the campaign across many accounts so that each account stays under the per-account threshold and the aggregate attack is reassembled from individually compliant accounts. If the per-account limit is a thousand requests an hour, the attacker uses fifty accounts at nine hundred requests an hour each, and every account is individually within bounds while the campaign runs at forty-five thousand requests an hour. Per-account detection sees fifty compliant accounts and nothing wrong. Detecting distributed abuse requires aggregating above the account. The campaign that is invisible per account becomes visible when the accounts are considered together, and the key is to find the shared attribute that links them. Distributed campaigns typically share something: a source IP or IP range, a credential or API key family, a creation-time cohort, a behavioural fingerprint, or a coverage pattern that, combined across accounts, sweeps the input space the way a single enumerator would. Aggregating the rate signal per source, per credential, and per behavioural cluster -- not only per account -- surfaces the campaign whose total rate is anomalous even though no member account's rate is. This is where the entity family begins to point toward the graph family. Distributed-abuse detection by shared attribute is a coarse, attribute-keyed version of the relationship analysis that 13.11 develops fully: linking accounts by shared infrastructure and synchronised behaviour to find the coordinated network. Rate-based detection handles the cases where a single shared attribute -- a common source, a common credential family -- reassembles the campaign, and hands the harder cases, where the linkage is structural rather than a single shared field, to the graph detector. The rate- based view of distributed abuse is the aggregate rate across a linking attribute; the graph view is the full relationship structure. Together they cover the distributed campaign from the simple shared- source case to the sophisticated structurally-coordinated one.

Part 6

Title

The Handoff to Smart Rate Limiting

Body

Rate-based detection produces a signal; it does not, by itself, act on it. The action -- throttling, challenging, or blocking the abusive entity -- is the job of the smart rate limiter in 13.08, and the relationship between the two sections is the organising handoff of this part. Detection sets the adaptive threshold; enforcement applies it. The handoff matters because the alternative -- a static rate limit applied without detection -- fails in the ways the next section opens with. A static limit set globally is simultaneously too loose for the low-and-slow campaign that stays under it and too tight for the legitimate power user that exceeds it. The detection built in this section is what makes the limit adaptive: the per-entity rate baseline defines what is normal for each entity, the rate, acceleration, coverage, and periodicity signals identify which entities are abusing, and these together set a per-entity adaptive threshold that the limiter enforces. An entity whose rate, periodicity, and coverage all indicate automated enumeration gets a tight limit; a legitimate power user with an irregular, clustered, human pattern keeps a loose one, even at the same raw rate. The detection signal also flows beyond the limiter. It feeds the user profile of 13.10, where a history of rate-abuse signals contributes to the entity's persistent risk score, so that an entity with a record of enumeration is scored more suspiciously on future activity. And the distributed- abuse linkage feeds the graph detector of 13.11. Rate-based detection is thus both the producer of an enforcement threshold for the next section and a contributor to the entity and graph families that follow. Its output is a per-entity rate-abuse signal -- composed from rate, acceleration, coverage, and periodicity, aggregated per account and per linking attribute -- that is the input to enforcement, profiling, and graph analysis alike.

Title

Rate-Based Detector

Filename

rate_based_detector.py

Description

Aggregates the per-request event stream by entity over sliding windows and computes rate, acceleration, and cumulative volume, scoring against the per-entity baseline to detect both burst and sustained abuse. Implements the rate and acceleration detection from Section 13.07 Parts 1 and 2.

Implementation Notes

Python 3.12, single quotes throughout per book convention, standard library only

Code

# rate_based_detector.py
# Section 13.07: Rate-Based Detector (Rate, Acceleration, Volume)
# 20260621 | AI Security Mastery -- Chapter 13 by Raghav Dinesh
# Single quotes throughout per book convention

from collections import deque
from dataclasses import dataclass, field
from typing import Deque, Dict, List, Tuple


@dataclass
class RateWindow:
    window_s: float
    timestamps: Deque[float] = field(default_factory=deque)

    def add(self, ts: float) -> None:
        self.timestamps.append(ts)
        self._evict(ts)

    def _evict(self, now: float) -> None:
        while self.timestamps and now - self.timestamps[0] > self.window_s:
            self.timestamps.popleft()

    def rate(self, now: float) -> float:
        self._evict(now)
        return len(self.timestamps) / (self.window_s / 60.0)  # per minute


@dataclass
class EntityRateState:
    short: RateWindow
    long: RateWindow
    prev_rate: float = 0.0
    cumulative: int = 0


@dataclass
class RateVerdict:
    rate_per_min: float
    acceleration: float
    cumulative: int
    burst: bool
    sustained: bool


class RateBasedDetector:
    def __init__(self, baseline_rate_per_min: float, short_s: float = 60, long_s: float = 3600) -> None:
        self.baseline = baseline_rate_per_min
        self.short_s = short_s
        self.long_s = long_s
        self.states: Dict[str, EntityRateState] = {}

    def _state(self, entity: str) -> EntityRateState:
        if entity not in self.states:
            self.states[entity] = EntityRateState(RateWindow(self.short_s), RateWindow(self.long_s))
        return self.states[entity]

    def observe(self, entity: str, ts: float) -> RateVerdict:
        st = self._state(entity)
        st.short.add(ts)
        st.long.add(ts)
        st.cumulative += 1

        rate = st.short.rate(ts)
        accel = rate - st.prev_rate
        st.prev_rate = rate

        long_rate = st.long.rate(ts)
        burst = rate > 5 * self.baseline and accel > 2 * self.baseline
        # sustained: long-window rate elevated and large cumulative volume
        sustained = long_rate > 1.5 * self.baseline and st.cumulative > 500

        return RateVerdict(round(rate, 2), round(accel, 2), st.cumulative, burst, sustained)


if __name__ == '__main__':
    det = RateBasedDetector(baseline_rate_per_min=2.0)
    base = 1_000_000.0

    # Burst: 100 requests in 30 seconds
    last = None
    for i in range(100):
        last = det.observe('user_burst', base + i * 0.3)
    print('Burst entity:', last)

    # Sustained: steady modest rate over a long period, high volume
    det2 = RateBasedDetector(baseline_rate_per_min=2.0)
    last2 = None
    for i in range(600):
        last2 = det2.observe('user_sustained', base + i * 6.0)  # ~10/min for an hour
    print('Sustained entity:', last2)
    print('Burst trips on rate+acceleration; sustained trips on long-window rate+volume.')

Title

Coverage Pattern Detector

Filename

coverage_pattern_detector.py

Description

Detects systematic input-space enumeration by measuring the breadth and uniformity of an entity coverage across input-space buckets, distinguishing a methodical sweep from the clustered pattern of legitimate use. Implements the coverage-pattern detection from Section 13.07 Part 3.

Implementation Notes

Python 3.12, single quotes throughout per book convention, standard library only; input-space position is abstracted as a bucket id supplied per request

Code

# coverage_pattern_detector.py
# Section 13.07: Coverage Pattern Detector (Enumeration Sweep)
# 20260621 | AI Security Mastery -- Chapter 13 by Raghav Dinesh
# Single quotes throughout per book convention

import math
from collections import Counter
from dataclasses import dataclass, field
from typing import Dict, List


@dataclass
class CoverageState:
    bucket_counts: Counter = field(default_factory=Counter)
    total: int = 0

    def add(self, bucket_id: int) -> None:
        self.bucket_counts[bucket_id] += 1
        self.total += 1


@dataclass
class CoverageVerdict:
    distinct_buckets: int
    breadth: float        # fraction of space covered
    uniformity: float     # normalised entropy of the coverage
    enumeration_suspected: bool


class CoveragePatternDetector:
    def __init__(self, space_size: int, breadth_threshold: float = 0.5, uniformity_threshold: float = 0.85) -> None:
        self.space_size = space_size
        self.breadth_threshold = breadth_threshold
        self.uniformity_threshold = uniformity_threshold
        self.states: Dict[str, CoverageState] = {}

    def observe(self, entity: str, bucket_id: int) -> CoverageVerdict:
        st = self.states.setdefault(entity, CoverageState())
        st.add(bucket_id)

        distinct = len(st.bucket_counts)
        breadth = distinct / self.space_size
        # normalised entropy: 1.0 = perfectly uniform coverage (sweep signature)
        if distinct > 1:
            probs = [c / st.total for c in st.bucket_counts.values()]
            entropy = -sum(p * math.log(p) for p in probs)
            uniformity = entropy / math.log(distinct)
        else:
            uniformity = 0.0

        suspected = breadth > self.breadth_threshold and uniformity > self.uniformity_threshold
        return CoverageVerdict(distinct, round(breadth, 3), round(uniformity, 3), suspected)


if __name__ == '__main__':
    # Legitimate user: returns to a few buckets repeatedly (clustered)
    legit = CoveragePatternDetector(space_size=100)
    import random
    random.seed(5)
    last_l = None
    for _ in range(200):
        last_l = legit.observe('user_legit', random.choice([3, 4, 5, 4, 3, 7]))
    print('Legitimate (clustered):', last_l)

    # Enumerator: sweeps the space uniformly and broadly
    enum = CoveragePatternDetector(space_size=100)
    last_e = None
    for b in range(100):
        last_e = enum.observe('user_enum', b)  # one query per bucket -- uniform sweep
    print('Enumerator (sweep):    ', last_e)
    print('The enumerator trips on high breadth and near-uniform coverage,')
    print('even though its per-request rate may be modest.')

Periodicity Analyser

Filename

periodicity_analyser.py

Description

Analyses the regularity of an entity inter-request intervals to detect the automation signature, using the coefficient of variation and a simple dominant-period check, robust to mild jitter. Implements the periodicity detection from Section 13.07 Part 4.

Implementation Notes

Python 3.12, single quotes throughout per book convention, standard library only

Code

# periodicity_analyser.py
# Section 13.07: Periodicity Analyser (Automation Signature)
# 20260621 | AI Security Mastery -- Chapter 13 by Raghav Dinesh
# Single quotes throughout per book convention

from dataclasses import dataclass
from typing import List


def _mean(values: List[float]) -> float:
    return sum(values) / len(values) if values else 0.0


def _std(values: List[float]) -> float:
    if len(values) < 2:
        return 0.0
    m = _mean(values)
    return (sum((v - m) ** 2 for v in values) / (len(values) - 1)) ** 0.5


@dataclass
class PeriodicityVerdict:
    n_intervals: int
    mean_interval: float
    cv: float                 # coefficient of variation
    automation_suspected: bool
    detail: str


def analyse_periodicity(timestamps: List[float], cv_threshold: float = 0.25) -> PeriodicityVerdict:
    if len(timestamps) < 5:
        return PeriodicityVerdict(0, 0.0, 0.0, False, 'insufficient data')
    ts = sorted(timestamps)
    intervals = [ts[i] - ts[i - 1] for i in range(1, len(ts))]
    m = _mean(intervals)
    cv = (_std(intervals) / m) if m > 0 else 0.0
    # Low CV = clockwork regularity = automation signature
    suspected = cv < cv_threshold
    detail = f'mean interval {m:.2f}s, CV {cv:.3f}'
    return PeriodicityVerdict(len(intervals), round(m, 3), round(cv, 3), suspected, detail)


if __name__ == '__main__':
    import random
    random.seed(8)
    base = 1000.0

    # Automated script: near-constant 5s interval (tiny jitter)
    script = [base + i * 5.0 + random.uniform(-0.1, 0.1) for i in range(60)]
    print('Automated script: ', analyse_periodicity(script))

    # Human: highly irregular intervals
    human_ts = [base]
    for _ in range(60):
        human_ts.append(human_ts[-1] + random.uniform(2, 90))
    print('Human user:       ', analyse_periodicity(human_ts))
    print('The script low CV is the automation signature; the human high CV is not.')

Title

Distributed Abuse Detector

Filename

distributed_abuse_detector.py

Description

Aggregates rate across accounts by shared linking attributes (source, credential, cohort) to surface a distributed campaign whose total rate is anomalous even though no member account exceeds its per-account limit. Implements the distributed-abuse detection from Section 13.07 Part 5.

Implementation Notes

Python 3.12, single quotes throughout per book convention, standard library only

Code

# distributed_abuse_detector.py
# Section 13.07: Distributed Abuse Detector (Cross-Account Aggregation)
# 20260621 | AI Security Mastery -- Chapter 13 by Raghav Dinesh
# Single quotes throughout per book convention

from collections import defaultdict
from dataclasses import dataclass, field
from typing import Dict, List, Set, Tuple


@dataclass
class AccountRequest:
    account_id: str
    source_ip: str
    credential_family: str
    rate_per_min: float


@dataclass
class CampaignVerdict:
    linking_attribute: str
    linking_value: str
    member_accounts: int
    aggregate_rate: float
    per_account_max: float
    campaign_suspected: bool


class DistributedAbuseDetector:
    def __init__(self, per_account_limit: float, campaign_multiplier: float = 3.0) -> None:
        self.per_account_limit = per_account_limit
        self.campaign_multiplier = campaign_multiplier

    def detect(self, requests: List[AccountRequest]) -> List[CampaignVerdict]:
        verdicts = []
        for attr_name, key in (('source_ip', lambda r: r.source_ip), ('credential_family', lambda r: r.credential_family)):
            groups: Dict[str, List[AccountRequest]] = defaultdict(list)
            for r in requests:
                groups[key(r)].append(r)
            for value, members in groups.items():
                accounts = {m.account_id for m in members}
                agg_rate = sum(m.rate_per_min for m in members)
                per_acct_max = max(m.rate_per_min for m in members)
                # Campaign: each account compliant, aggregate far over the limit
                suspected = (
                    per_acct_max <= self.per_account_limit
                    and agg_rate > self.campaign_multiplier * self.per_account_limit
                    and len(accounts) >= 3
                )
                if suspected:
                    verdicts.append(CampaignVerdict(
                        attr_name, value, len(accounts),
                        round(agg_rate, 1), round(per_acct_max, 1), True,
                    ))
        return verdicts


if __name__ == '__main__':
    limit = 100.0
    # 5 accounts, each at 90/min (under the 100 limit), all from one source range
    reqs = [AccountRequest(f'acct_{i}', '203.0.113.10', 'keyfam_A', 90.0) for i in range(5)]
    # plus some unrelated legitimate accounts
    reqs += [AccountRequest('acct_x', '198.51.100.4', 'keyfam_B', 30.0)]

    det = DistributedAbuseDetector(per_account_limit=limit)
    for v in det.detect(reqs):
        print(f'Campaign on {v.linking_attribute}={v.linking_value}: '
              f'{v.member_accounts} accounts, aggregate {v.aggregate_rate}/min, '
              f'per-account max {v.per_account_max}/min (limit {limit})')
    print('Each account is under the per-account limit, but the aggregate over')
    print('the shared source reveals the distributed campaign.')

Common Mistakes

Mistake 1

Description

Relying on per-request detection for exfiltration and enumeration, which have no per-request signal

Why It Happens

The Chapter 12 per-request classifiers are the primary detection investment, and they catch the attacks that have a per-request signal well. It is easy to assume they cover all attacks, and an exfiltration campaign of benign-looking queries does not obviously announce that it needs a different detection layer.

Consequence

Exfiltration and enumeration consist of individually benign queries -- each is well-formed, on-topic, and legitimate in isolation -- so every per-request detector passes every query correctly. The attack exists only in the aggregate volume, rate, and coverage, which no per-request detector computes. The entire class of rate-based abuse is invisible, and the system has no idea it is being systematically queried to reconstruct a model or extract a dataset.

Correct Approach

Aggregate the per-request event stream by entity over time and detect on the aggregate features -- rate, acceleration, cumulative volume, coverage, periodicity -- that only exist at the entity-over- time level. rate_based_detector.py and coverage_pattern_detector.py compute these aggregate signals that per-request detection structurally cannot.

Mistake 2

Description

Thresholding only the instantaneous rate, missing both the sustained slow campaign and the burst onset

Why It Happens

Instantaneous request rate is the obvious rate feature, and a single threshold on it is simple to configure. It catches the loud high-rate floods, which are the most visible rate abuse, so it feels like adequate rate detection.

Consequence

The two main rate-abuse signatures both evade an instantaneous-rate threshold. The sustained slow campaign keeps its rate modestly above baseline -- below a threshold tuned for floods -- but runs for hours, accumulating an enormous total volume that the instantaneous rate never reflects. And a burst is caught only after its volume has accumulated, not at onset, because the threshold reacts to rate, not its rate of change. The detector catches the obvious flood and misses the patient campaign and the early burst.

Correct Approach

Monitor rate, acceleration, and cumulative volume together. Acceleration catches the burst at onset before the full volume accumulates; cumulative volume over a long window catches the sustained campaign that never spikes. rate_based_detector.py tracks all three so both burst and sustained abuse are covered.

Mistake 3

Description

Treating a high request rate as proof of abuse and throttling legitimate power users

Why It Happens

High rate is the most intuitive abuse signal, and an entity making far more requests than average looks suspicious. Acting on rate alone is the straightforward response, and it does catch real abuse.

Consequence

Legitimate power users -- heavy integrations, batch consumers, busy analysts -- make many requests at a high rate as a matter of normal operation. Treating rate alone as abuse throttles them, breaking legitimate high-volume use to defend against abuse, and floods the alert stream with false positives on the system's best customers. Rate without context cannot distinguish the power user from the exfiltration script.

Correct Approach

Combine rate with the features that distinguish automated abuse from human heavy use: periodicity (the script regular, the human irregular) and coverage (the enumerator broad sweep, the user clustered pattern). Two entities at the same rate are distinguished by these -- the regular, broad-sweeping one is abuse, the irregular, clustered one is a power user. periodicity_analyser.py and coverage_pattern_detector.py provide the distinguishing context.

Mistake 4

Description

Applying only per-account rate detection, leaving distributed campaigns across many accounts undetected

Why It Happens

Per-account rate limiting and detection is the standard, well-understood approach, and it works against the unsophisticated attacker using one account. Once per-account detection is in place, the rate problem feels solved.

Consequence

The sophisticated adversary distributes the campaign across many accounts, each staying under the per-account threshold, so the aggregate attack is reassembled from individually compliant accounts. Per-account detection sees a set of compliant accounts and nothing wrong, while the campaign runs at many times the per-account limit in total. The per-account approach is exactly the constraint the distributed attack is designed to satisfy.

Correct Approach

Aggregate the rate signal above the account -- per source, per credential family, per cohort -- to surface the campaign whose total rate is anomalous even though no member account exceeds its limit. distributed_abuse_detector.py aggregates by shared linking attributes, and the harder structurally- coordinated cases hand off to the graph detector of 13.11.

Mistake 5

Description

Building a naive periodicity detector that looks only for near-constant intervals and is defeated by simple jitter

Why It Happens

The cleanest automation signature is a perfectly regular interval, and a detector that flags near-constant spacing catches the unsophisticated script. It is the most direct implementation of the periodicity idea and works against naive automation.

Consequence

A sophisticated attacker adds jitter -- randomising the intervals to mimic human irregularity -- which defeats a detector that looks only for near-constant spacing. The jittered script passes the periodicity check while still operating as automation, and a periodicity detector relied on alone is evaded by a cheap modification.

Correct Approach

Treat periodicity as one signal among several rather than a sole detector, and look for structure beyond simple regularity -- a dominant periodic component, a burst-and-pause rhythm, or a coverage pattern that persists despite jitter. Each individual signal can be evaded, but evading rate, acceleration, coverage, and periodicity simultaneously is expensive, so the composite is robust where any single feature is not. periodicity_analyser.py contributes to the composite rather than standing alone.

Section Summary

Compact

Rate-based abuse is the purest case of an attack with no per-request signal: exfiltration and enumeration campaigns consist of individually benign queries, and the entire signal lives in the aggregate rate, acceleration, cumulative volume, coverage, and periodicity that only exist when the per-request event stream is aggregated by entity over time. Section 13.07 opens the entity family by building these aggregate detectors. Rate and acceleration catch the burst at onset; cumulative volume over a long window catches the sustained slow campaign that never spikes. Coverage-pattern detection catches systematic input-space enumeration -- the broad, uniform sweep that distinguishes an enumerator from the clustered pattern of legitimate use -- and catches the patient enumerator who keeps rate modest to evade rate thresholds. Periodicity detection identifies the automation signature in regular inter- request intervals, distinguishing an exfiltration script from a heavy-but-human power user at the same rate, though it must be combined with the other features to resist jitter. Distributed-abuse detection aggregates above the account -- per source, per credential, per cohort -- to reassemble the campaign spread across many individually-compliant accounts, pointing toward the graph family. The composite rate-abuse signal feeds the user profile of 13.10, the graph detector of 13.11, and above all the adaptive rate limiter of 13.08: detection sets the per-entity adaptive threshold, enforcement applies it.

Key Takeaways

  • Exfiltration and enumeration have no per-request signal: every query is individually benign and the entire attack lives in the aggregate rate, volume, coverage, and periodicity computed per entity over time
  • Rate and acceleration catch the burst at onset while cumulative volume over a long window catches the sustained slow campaign -- thresholding instantaneous rate alone misses both the slow campaign and the early burst
  • Coverage-pattern detection catches systematic enumeration through the broad, uniform sweep that distinguishes an enumerator from the clustered pattern of legitimate use, even when rate is kept modest
  • Periodicity is the automation signature -- regular intervals are a script tell humans do not produce -- and it distinguishes an exfiltration script from a heavy-but-human power user at the same rate
  • Per-account detection invites distribution across many compliant accounts, so detection must aggregate above the account by source, credential, and cohort to reassemble the campaign, pointing toward the graph family
  • Rate-based detection and rate limiting are a detection-enforcement pair: detection composes the per-entity rate-abuse signal and sets the adaptive threshold, and the smart rate limiter of 13.08 applies it

Summary
