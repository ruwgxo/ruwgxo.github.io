Section Overview

One Line Summary

Coverage measurement transforms a list of ATLAS techniques and Sigma rules into a scored, prioritised, and communicable detection posture — and coverage drift tracking ensures that posture does not silently degrade as new techniques emerge and existing rules evolve.

Why This Section Exists

Section 11.04 named every ATLAS technique relevant to the seven-track gateway and established the technique-to-detection chain for each. That inventory is precise but static: it tells you what is covered but not how well, not what is most urgent to fix, and not whether the programme is improving or degrading over time. Section 11.05 converts the inventory into a living measurement system. Coverage scores make quality explicit — partial is not the same as full and the score makes that visible. Gap prioritisation converts the backlog from an unordered list into an ordered one: high-likelihood, high-impact gaps first; theoretical but low-probability techniques last. Coverage drift tracking catches silent regression: a rule calibration that reduces false positives but also reduces true positive coverage degrades the coverage score without any engineer noticing unless the measurement runs on a schedule. The two deliverables in this section — the coverage heat map generator and the gap prioritiser — are operational tools used throughout Chapters 12 and 13. Every new detector those chapters build is evaluated against the gap prioritiser backlog to confirm it closes the intended gap before being merged to main.

Position In Chapter

Section 11.05 closes the measurement loop opened in Section 11.01 (coverage without measurement is a debt category) and operationalises the technique inventory from Section 11.04. Together, Sections 11.04 and 11.05 form the coverage measurement subsystem that Sections 11.06 through 11.13 build rules and detectors into.

Prose Content

Part 1 Coverage Not Binary

Heading

Coverage Is Not Binary

Body

The most common error in detection coverage reporting is treating coverage as a binary property: a technique is either covered or it is not. Binary coverage reporting produces misleading heat maps: a technique with one Sigma rule that fires on 30% of attack variants appears green alongside a technique with comprehensive multi-layer detection that fires on 95% of variants. The difference in actual defensive value is enormous; the heat map shows none. The three-tier coverage model introduced in Section 11.04 and operationalised here makes coverage quality explicit: Score 0 — No detection. No rule or detector fires on any known variant of this technique. The technique has an actionable gap: evidence exists at inference time but no detection artefact captures it. For intentional gaps (no runtime evidence), the score is recorded separately rather than as 0, to avoid inflating the actionable backlog. Score 1 — Partial detection. At least one rule fires on at least one variant of the technique, but confirmed variants exist that evade all deployed rules. Examples from the gateway: AML.T0051 scores 1 because Track 1 detects direct injection but injection unlike anything in its training data evades Track 1's keywords, centroid gate and classifier. AML.T0040 scores 1 because Track 6 detects per-user extraction but distributed extraction across unlinked accounts evades the per-user query monitor and the cross-user correlator. Score 2 — Full detection. All known variants of the technique are covered by deployed rules at one or more detection layers. No confirmed evasion variant exists in the current threat model. Full coverage is a time-bound claim: it is valid as of the date the assessment was run against the current threat intelligence for that technique. A technique at score 2 today may regress to score 1 when a new evasion variant is published. The score is computed per technique, not per rule. A technique with three partial rules that together cover 95% of variants is still score 1 if a confirmed evasion variant exists. Moving from score 1 to score 2 requires closing the specific gap named in the residual_gap field of the ATLASTechnique registry, not simply adding more rules.

Part 2 Heatmap Construction

Heading

Heat Map Construction: From Rule Inventory to Visual Coverage

Body

The heat map is built in three steps: rule inventory collection, technique score computation, and visualisation export. Step 1 — Rule inventory collection. The attck_coverage_heatmap.py deliverable reads the mitre_technique fields from all deployed Sigma rules via rule_metadata_validator.py and cross-references them against the ATLASTechnique registry from atlas_attack_tree_mapper.py. Each rule contributes its technique IDs to the coverage record for those techniques. A rule with mitre_technique: [AML.T0051, AML.T0043] contributes to two technique coverage records simultaneously. Step 2 — Technique score computation. For each technique in the ATLAS registry, the scorer looks at the deployed rules contributing to that technique and applies the scoring logic: if residual_gap is non-empty and chain_complete is False, the technique cannot score above 1 regardless of how many rules cover it. Score 2 requires both chain_complete: True and an explicit full_coverage_date set by the detection engineer after confirming no evasion variants exist. This prevents rules from automatically promoting themselves to score 2 without human sign-off. Step 3 — Visualisation export. The attck_navigator_export.py from Section 11.04 accepts the score dictionary from attck_coverage_heatmap.py and generates the Navigator-compatible JSON. The heat map can also be rendered as an ASCII table for terminal output during CI runs — a fast sanity check that does not require loading the Navigator UI. Two complementary views of the same data serve two audiences. The Navigator heat map is the leadership communication artefact: one slide, colour-coded by technique. The ASCII coverage table is the engineering working view: technique ID, score, rule count, residual gap, and next action in a format that fits a terminal window and can be diffed between runs.

Part 3 Gap Analysis

Heading

Gap Analysis: Prioritising the Detection Backlog

Body

Not all gaps are equal. An uncovered technique with a high probability of being used against the organisation and a high impact if it succeeds is a P0 backlog item. An uncovered technique that is theoretically possible but requires physical access to hardware and has no known exploitation in the wild is a low-priority item that can wait for a future quarter. The gap prioritisation score is: priority = likelihood × impact. Both dimensions are scored on a 1–5 scale. Likelihood reflects the probability that the technique will be used against this specific deployment: base rate from threat intelligence, exposure from the deployment profile (public API vs internal tool, model type, user base), and known active campaigns using this technique. Impact reflects the consequences if the technique succeeds: model integrity, data confidentiality, availability of the service, and regulatory or reputational downstream effects. For the seven-track gateway, the six highest-priority actionable gaps in likelihood × impact order: Gap 1: AML.T0051 obfuscated injection variants (L3 gap). Likelihood: 5 — public APIs are actively probed with obfuscated injections. Impact: 5 — successful injection can override model behaviour entirely. Priority score: 25. Remediation: Chapter 12 intent classifier (Section 12.04). Gap 2: AML.T0054 novel jailbreak framings (L3 gap). Likelihood: 5 — novel jailbreak templates are published continuously and tested against public LLM APIs. Impact: 4 — jailbreak enables policy bypass; may not cause full system compromise. Priority: 20. Remediation: Chapter 12 adversarial training of jailbreak classifier (Section 12.03). Gap 3: AML.T0040 distributed extraction (L4 gap). Likelihood: 3 — coordinated multi-account extraction requires significant attacker investment. Impact: 5 — successful extraction reconstructs model weights or decision boundaries, enabling evasion and IP theft. Priority: 15. Remediation: Chapter 13 cross-user correlation (Section 13.03). Gap 4: AML.T0043 novel adversarial families (L3 gap). Likelihood: 3 — novel adversarial attack families require research capability but new families are published regularly. Impact: 4 — successful adversarial input causes targeted misclassification. Priority: 12. Remediation: Chapter 12 expanded adversarial classifier training (Section 12.05). Gap 5: AML.T0020 slow-drift poisoning (L4 gap). Likelihood: 2 — slow-drift poisoning requires sustained access to the training pipeline; rare but high-value for targeted attacks. Impact: 5 — subtle model behaviour corruption is persistent and hard to detect post-deployment. Priority: 10. Remediation: Chapter 13 training data drift monitor (Section 13.04). Gap 6: AML.T0019 inference-time backdoor trigger (undetected). Likelihood: 2 — requires training pipeline access for insertion; inference trigger is rare in practice. Impact: 4 — backdoor activates on specific input to produce attacker- controlled outputs. Priority: 8. Remediation: future track — output consistency monitor not yet scheduled. The gap prioritiser enforces this ordering programmatically. When a Chapter 12 or 13 section delivers a new detector, the gap prioritiser is re-run to confirm the targeted gap has closed (technique score improves from 0 or 1 to 1 or 2) and to update the backlog ordering for remaining gaps.

Part 4 Coverage Drift

Heading

Coverage Drift: How Heat Maps Degrade and How to Catch It

Body

A detection programme that measures coverage once and never re-measures has a static picture of a dynamic problem. Coverage drifts downward through three mechanisms. Mechanism 1 — Rule regression. A Sigma rule is calibrated to reduce false positives by raising a threshold or tightening a keyword list. The calibration reduces alert noise as intended but also reduces true positive coverage: the tighter threshold misses obfuscated attack variants that the original threshold caught. The coverage score for the affected technique regresses from partial to lower partial. Without scheduled coverage measurement, the regression is invisible until a real attack evades the calibrated rule. Detection: the quarterly coverage run computes coverage scores from the current deployed rules and diffs them against the previous quarter. A technique score that decreased triggers a regression alert with the rule ID and commit hash of the calibration that caused it. Mechanism 2 — Technique taxonomy expansion. MITRE ATLAS adds new techniques as new adversarial ML research matures. A technique published after the last coverage assessment appears as neither covered nor gapped in the current heat map — it is simply absent, which is the worst kind of gap: not visible. Without a scheduled ATLAS version check, new techniques accumulate unnoticed. Detection: the quarterly coverage run fetches the current ATLAS technique list and diffs it against the technique list used in the previous run. New techniques appear as automatic score-0 entries in the heat map with a flag indicating they are new additions not yet assessed. Mechanism 3 — Attack variant evolution. A technique at score 2 (full coverage) can regress when adversaries publish new evasion variants that bypass all deployed rules. This is the hardest drift mechanism to detect programmatically because it requires threat intelligence — awareness that a new variant exists — not just rule inspection. Detection: the quarterly coverage run includes a threat intelligence step: reviewing published adversarial ML research and exploit releases from the previous quarter for new variants of covered techniques. Any new variant that evades deployed rules is added as a regression test case (Section 11.02) and the technique score is downgraded accordingly. The quarterly cadence is the minimum defensible review frequency. Programmes with high-value public APIs (where active probing is continuous) should run coverage measurement monthly. The attck_coverage_heatmap.py deliverable is designed to run in CI — a coverage check can run weekly as part of the nightly test suite, with alerting only when a score regresses.

#!/usr/bin/env python3
'''attck_coverage_heatmap.py
Programmatic ATLAS coverage heat map for the seven-track gateway.
Computes per-technique scores, detects regression vs previous run,
checks for new ATLAS techniques, outputs ASCII table and Navigator JSON.
Chapter 11, Section 11.05 — AI Security Mastery
Author: Raghav Dinesh | github: ruwgxo

Usage:
  python3 attck_coverage_heatmap.py
  python3 attck_coverage_heatmap.py --diff previous_scores.json
  python3 attck_coverage_heatmap.py --save scores.json
  python3 attck_coverage_heatmap.py --check-new-techniques previous_techniques.json
'''

from __future__ import annotations
import argparse
import json
import pathlib
import sys
from dataclasses import dataclass
from datetime import datetime, timezone

from atlas_attack_tree_mapper import (
    TECHNIQUES,
    CoverageStatus,
    coverage_score,
)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Score model
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

@dataclass
class TechniqueScore:
    technique_id: str
    name: str
    score: int          # 0, 1, 2, or -1 for intentional gap
    intentional: bool
    residual_gap: str
    actionable_next_step: str
    assessed_at: str

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Score computation
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

def compute_scores(
    techniques: list,
    full_coverage_overrides: dict[str, bool] | None = None,
) -> list[TechniqueScore]:
    '''
    Compute coverage scores for all techniques.
    full_coverage_overrides: dict of technique_id -> True for manually confirmed
    score-2 promotions (requires chain_complete = True in the registry).
    '''
    overrides = full_coverage_overrides or {}
    now = datetime.now(timezone.utc).isoformat()
    scores: list[TechniqueScore] = []

    for t in techniques:
        base = coverage_score(t.coverage_status)
        intentional = t.coverage_status == CoverageStatus.NONE_INTENTIONAL

        # Score-2 requires explicit human sign-off via override and chain_complete
        if base == 2 and not (t.chain_complete and overrides.get(t.technique_id)):
            base = 1   # auto-cap at 1 unless chain complete and manually confirmed

        scores.append(TechniqueScore(
            technique_id=t.technique_id,
            name=t.name,
            score=base,
            intentional=intentional,
            residual_gap=t.residual_gap,
            actionable_next_step=t.actionable_next_step,
            assessed_at=now,
        ))

    return scores

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Regression diff
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

@dataclass
class ScoreDelta:
    technique_id: str
    name: str
    previous: int
    current: int
    direction: str    # 'improved' | 'regressed' | 'unchanged'

def diff_scores(
    current: list[TechniqueScore],
    previous_path: pathlib.Path,
) -> list[ScoreDelta]:
    with open(previous_path) as f:
        prev_data: dict[str, int] = json.load(f)

    deltas: list[ScoreDelta] = []
    for s in current:
        prev_score = prev_data.get(s.technique_id, -99)
        if prev_score == -99:
            continue  # new technique; not a regression
        if s.score > prev_score:
            direction = 'improved'
        elif s.score < prev_score:
            direction = 'regressed'
        else:
            direction = 'unchanged'
        deltas.append(ScoreDelta(
            technique_id=s.technique_id,
            name=s.name,
            previous=prev_score,
            current=s.score,
            direction=direction,
        ))
    return deltas

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# New technique check
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

def check_new_techniques(
    current: list[TechniqueScore],
    previous_techniques_path: pathlib.Path,
) -> list[str]:
    '''Returns technique IDs present in current inventory but absent from previous run.'''
    with open(previous_techniques_path) as f:
        prev_ids: list[str] = json.load(f)
    current_ids = {s.technique_id for s in current}
    return sorted(current_ids - set(prev_ids))

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# ASCII output
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

SCORE_LABELS = {-1: 'INTENTL', 0: 'NONE   ', 1: 'PARTIAL', 2: 'FULL   '}
SCORE_SYMBOLS = {-1: '◈', 0: '✗', 1: '◑', 2: '✓'}

def print_ascii_heatmap(scores: list[TechniqueScore]) -> None:
    actionable = [s for s in scores if not s.intentional and s.score < 2]
    full = [s for s in scores if s.score == 2]
    intentional = [s for s in scores if s.intentional]

    print('\nATLAS Coverage Heat Map — Seven-Track Gateway')
    print(f'Generated: {datetime.now(timezone.utc).strftime("%Y-%m-%d %H:%M UTC")}')
    print(f'Techniques assessed: {len(scores)}')
    print(f'  Full (✓):      {len(full)}')
    print(f'  Partial/None:  {len(actionable)}')
    print(f'  Intentional:   {len(intentional)}')
    print()
    print(f'{"ID":<14} {"Score":<10} {"Sym":<4} {"Name":<45} Gap / Status')
    print('-' * 120)
    for s in sorted(scores, key=lambda x: (-x.score if not x.intentional else -2, x.technique_id)):
        label = SCORE_LABELS.get(s.score, '?      ')
        sym = SCORE_SYMBOLS.get(s.score, '?')
        gap_short = (s.residual_gap[:45] + '...') if len(s.residual_gap) > 45 else s.residual_gap
        print(f'{s.technique_id:<14} {label:<10} {sym:<4} {s.name:<45} {gap_short}')
    print()

def print_regression_report(deltas: list[ScoreDelta]) -> int:
    regressions = [d for d in deltas if d.direction == 'regressed']
    improvements = [d for d in deltas if d.direction == 'improved']
    print(f'Coverage Diff Report')
    print(f'  Improved:  {len(improvements)}')
    print(f'  Regressed: {len(regressions)}')
    print(f'  Unchanged: {len([d for d in deltas if d.direction == "unchanged"])}')
    if regressions:
        print('\n  REGRESSIONS (investigate before merging):')
        for d in regressions:
            print(f'    {d.technique_id} ({d.name}): {d.previous} → {d.current}')
    if improvements:
        print('\n  Improvements:')
        for d in improvements:
            print(f'    {d.technique_id} ({d.name}): {d.previous} → {d.current}')
    print()
    return 1 if regressions else 0

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Save scores for future diff
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

def save_scores(scores: list[TechniqueScore], path: pathlib.Path) -> None:
    data = {s.technique_id: s.score for s in scores}
    with open(path, 'w') as f:
        json.dump(data, f, indent=2)
    print(f'Scores saved to {path} for future diff.')

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Entry point
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

def main() -> int:
    parser = argparse.ArgumentParser(description='ATLAS coverage heat map generator')
    parser.add_argument('--diff', metavar='PREV_SCORES_JSON',
                        help='Diff current scores against previous run JSON')
    parser.add_argument('--save', metavar='OUTPUT_JSON',
                        help='Save current scores to JSON for future diff')
    parser.add_argument('--check-new-techniques', metavar='PREV_TECHNIQUES_JSON',
                        help='Report technique IDs added since previous run')
    args = parser.parse_args()

    # Compute current scores
    # Score-2 overrides would go here after human sign-off; none yet in Part 2
    scores = compute_scores(TECHNIQUES, full_coverage_overrides={})
    print_ascii_heatmap(scores)

    exit_code = 0

    if args.diff:
        prev_path = pathlib.Path(args.diff)
        if not prev_path.exists():
            print(f'Previous scores file not found: {prev_path}')
        else:
            deltas = diff_scores(scores, prev_path)
            regression_code = print_regression_report(deltas)
            exit_code = max(exit_code, regression_code)

    if args.check_new_techniques:
        prev_path = pathlib.Path(args.check_new_techniques)
        if prev_path.exists():
            new_ids = check_new_techniques(scores, prev_path)
            if new_ids:
                print(f'New ATLAS techniques detected (add to backlog):')
                for tid in new_ids:
                    print(f'  {tid}')
                print()

    if args.save:
        save_scores(scores, pathlib.Path(args.save))

    return exit_code

if __name__ == '__main__':
    sys.exit(main())

from __future__ import annotations
import argparse
import sys
from dataclasses import dataclass

from atlas_attack_tree_mapper import TECHNIQUES, CoverageStatus, coverage_score

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Likelihood and impact registry
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Scored 1-5: likelihood = probability of use against this deployment type
# (public LLM API, Chapter 11 reference deployment); impact = consequence
# if technique succeeds. Scores are fixed for the gateway reference threat model.

LIKELIHOOD: dict[str, int] = {
    'AML.T0051': 5,   # Active probing of public LLM APIs is continuous
    'AML.T0054': 5,   # Novel jailbreak templates released regularly and tested immediately
    'AML.T0043': 3,   # Requires adversarial ML capability; targeted deployments
    'AML.T0015': 3,   # Shared likelihood with AML.T0043
    'AML.T0020': 2,   # Requires training pipeline access; rare but high-value
    'AML.T0019': 2,   # Requires training pipeline access for insertion
    'AML.T0040': 3,   # Model extraction is well-documented; monetised in underground markets
    'AML.T0043': 2,   # Camera-facing deployments only; limited exposure
    'AML.T0017': 1,   # Staging is attacker-controlled; not measurable
    'AML.T0024.000': 2,   # Membership inference is research-grade; rare in production
    'AML.T0024': 1,   # Model inversion requires many queries; detectable via rate limiting
}

IMPACT: dict[str, int] = {
    'AML.T0051': 5,   # Full model instruction override
    'AML.T0054': 4,   # Policy bypass; may not cause full system compromise
    'AML.T0043': 4,   # Targeted misclassification; model integrity
    'AML.T0015': 4,   # Evasion outcome; same impact as AML.T0043
    'AML.T0020': 5,   # Persistent model behaviour corruption; hard to remediate
    'AML.T0019': 4,   # Trigger-controlled output; persistent
    'AML.T0040': 5,   # IP theft; evasion toolkit construction from stolen model
    'AML.T0043': 3,   # Physical domain; limited to camera-facing deployments
    'AML.T0017': 1,   # Staging; no direct impact at inference time
    'AML.T0024.000': 3,   # Training data privacy; regulatory and compliance impact
    'AML.T0024': 3,   # Training data reconstruction; privacy and IP impact
}

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Data model
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

@dataclass
class GapItem:
    technique_id: str
    name: str
    current_score: int
    likelihood: int
    impact: int
    priority: int
    residual_gap: str
    actionable_next_step: str
    intentional: bool

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Prioritiser
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

def build_backlog(techniques: list) -> list[GapItem]:
    items: list[GapItem] = []
    for t in techniques:
        score = coverage_score(t.coverage_status)
        intentional = t.coverage_status == CoverageStatus.NONE_INTENTIONAL
        likelihood = LIKELIHOOD.get(t.technique_id, 1)
        impact = IMPACT.get(t.technique_id, 1)
        priority = likelihood * impact
        items.append(GapItem(
            technique_id=t.technique_id,
            name=t.name,
            current_score=max(score, 0),
            likelihood=likelihood,
            impact=impact,
            priority=priority,
            residual_gap=t.residual_gap,
            actionable_next_step=t.actionable_next_step,
            intentional=intentional,
        ))
    # Sort: intentional gaps last, then by priority descending, then by score ascending
    items.sort(key=lambda x: (x.intentional, -x.priority, x.current_score))
    return items

def print_backlog(items: list[GapItem]) -> None:
    actionable = [i for i in items if not i.intentional and i.current_score < 2]
    intentional = [i for i in items if i.intentional]
    full = [i for i in items if i.current_score == 2]

    print('\nDetection Gap Prioritisation Backlog')
    print(f'Actionable gaps: {len(actionable)}')
    print(f'Full coverage:   {len(full)}')
    print(f'Intentional:     {len(intentional)}')
    print()
    print(f'{"#":<4} {"Technique":<14} {"Score":<7} {"L":<4} {"I":<4} {"P=LxI":<8} {"Name":<40} Next Step')
    print('-' * 140)

    rank = 1
    for item in items:
        if item.intentional:
            label = 'INTENTL'
            next_step_short = 'No action required.'
        elif item.current_score == 2:
            label = 'FULL   '
            next_step_short = 'Maintain; re-assess quarterly.'
        else:
            label = f'{item.current_score}/2    '
            ns = item.actionable_next_step
            next_step_short = (ns[:55] + '...') if len(ns) > 55 else ns

        name_short = (item.name[:38] + '..') if len(item.name) > 38 else item.name
        l_str = str(item.likelihood) if not item.intentional else '-'
        i_str = str(item.impact) if not item.intentional else '-'
        p_str = str(item.priority) if not item.intentional else '-'

        print(f'{rank:<4} {item.technique_id:<14} {label:<7} {l_str:<4} {i_str:<4} {p_str:<8} {name_short:<40} {next_step_short}')
        rank += 1
    print()

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Post-build validator
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

def validate_gap_closure(
    technique_ids: list[str],
    items: list[GapItem],
) -> int:
    '''
    Given a list of technique IDs that a newly built detector claims to cover,
    confirms whether the targeted gaps have improved in the current backlog.
    Returns 0 (all gaps improved or closed) or 1 (some gaps not improved).
    '''
    print(f'\nPost-Build Gap Closure Validation')
    print(f'Validating techniques: {", ".join(technique_ids)}\n')
    exit_code = 0
    by_id = {i.technique_id: i for i in items}
    for tid in technique_ids:
        item = by_id.get(tid)
        if item is None:
            print(f'  UNKNOWN  {tid}: not in ATLAS technique registry.')
            exit_code = 1
            continue
        if item.intentional:
            print(f'  SKIP     {tid}: intentional gap — no detector expected.')
            continue
        if item.current_score == 2:
            print(f'  FULL     {tid} ({item.name}): already at score 2. No gap to close.')
            continue
        # Score < 2 and not intentional: gap exists; detector claims to address it
        # In production: re-run coverage score with new detector in place and compare
        print(f'  GAP [{item.current_score}/2] {tid} ({item.name}):')
        print(f'     Residual gap: {item.residual_gap[:80]}')
        print(f'     Expected improvement: score {item.current_score} → {item.current_score + 1}')
        print(f'     Confirm by running: attck_coverage_heatmap.py --diff <prev_scores.json>')
    return exit_code

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Entry point
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

def main() -> int:
    parser = argparse.ArgumentParser(description='Detection gap prioritiser')
    parser.add_argument(
        '--validate', nargs='+', metavar='TECHNIQUE_ID',
        help='Validate that a new detector closes the specified technique gaps',
    )
    args = parser.parse_args()

    items = build_backlog(TECHNIQUES)
    print_backlog(items)

    if args.validate:
        return validate_gap_closure(args.validate, items)

    return 0

if __name__ == '__main__':
    sys.exit(main())

Common Mistakes

Id

CM11.05.1

Mistake

Promoting a technique from score 1 to score 2 automatically when a new rule is added

Why It Happens

The natural instinct is that adding a rule improves coverage, so the score should increase. Automating the promotion feels like a reward for building the new rule.

Why It Matters

Score 2 is a claim that all known variants of a technique are covered. That claim requires human verification that the new rule closes the specific residual gap documented in the ATLASTechnique registry, not just that a new rule fires on some inputs. A rule that adds partial coverage on top of existing partial coverage should produce a better score-1 entry, not a score-2 entry.

Correct Approach

Score-2 promotion requires two conditions: chain_complete is set to True in the ATLASTechnique registry by a detection engineer who has confirmed no evasion variants exist, and a full_coverage_override is passed to compute_scores() in attck_coverage_heatmap.py. Both require human action. The pipeline cannot auto-promote.

Id

CM11.05.2

Mistake

Building the gap backlog by priority score alone, ignoring sequential dependencies between gaps

Why It Happens

The priority score produces a clear ordered list. Working from the top feels methodical and data-driven.

Why It Matters

Some gaps have dependencies. AML.T0015 (Evade ML Model) shares its residual gap with AML.T0043 (Craft Adversarial Data): closing AML.T0043 closes AML.T0015 automatically. Building a separate detector for AML.T0015 wastes engineering effort. Similarly, the Chapter 12 intent classifier that closes AML.T0051 also reduces the residual gap for AML.T0054. Sequential dependencies mean the backlog ordering is a starting point, not a rigid prescription.

Correct Approach

Before beginning a detection sprint, check the ATLASTechnique registry for chain_gap_link fields that reference another technique. Shared-gap techniques should be batched into the same sprint as their parent gap. The gap prioritiser output includes the actionable_next_step field, which names the chapter section that addresses each gap — sprint planning uses this to batch related work.

Id

CM11.05.3

Mistake

Running the coverage heat map only once per quarter and not integrating it into CI

Why It Happens

The quarterly cadence is described as the minimum viable frequency. Teams interpret minimum as sufficient and do not add the coverage check to the weekly CI run.

Why It Matters

A rule calibration PR merged on Monday that causes a coverage regression will not be caught until the quarterly review — potentially weeks later. During that window, the detection programme reports score-1 coverage for a technique that is now effectively score-0 for obfuscated variants.

Correct Approach

Run attck_coverage_heatmap.py --diff in CI on every merge to main, in diff mode against the last stable scores JSON. Set the pipeline to fail if any technique score regresses without an explicit override in the PR. The quarterly review is for the manual steps (TI review, new technique check) that CI cannot automate.

Id

CM11.05.4

Mistake

Presenting the heat map to leadership without explaining the partial/full distinction

Why It Happens

Leadership audiences are not familiar with the three-tier scoring model. Amber cells look like progress; the nuance that amber means confirmed residual gap is lost.

Why It Matters

Leadership makes investment decisions based on the heat map. If six amber techniques are presented as "covered," the investment case for Chapter 12 and 13 detectors disappears. The team loses the resource allocation needed to close the highest- priority gaps.

Correct Approach

Always present the heat map with a legend slide that defines the three tiers. Lead with the amber cells and their residual gaps, not with the number of techniques that have any coverage. The narrative is: "We detect these six techniques partially; here is what an attacker can still do; here is the investment required to close it."

Id

CM11.05.5

Mistake

Assigning likelihood and impact scores once and never revising them

Why It Happens

Scoring threat likelihood and impact feels like a one-time calibration exercise. Once scored, the numbers go into the registry and are used without questioning.

Why It Matters

Threat likelihood changes as the threat landscape evolves. AML.T0040 (model extraction) had a moderate likelihood score two years ago; it is now a commercially available attack service. A likelihood score of 3 that should have been revised to 5 means the distributed extraction gap (currently Priority 15) is being treated as lower priority than it deserves.

Correct Approach

Review likelihood and impact scores quarterly as part of the same review cadence as the technique inventory. Use threat intelligence from the previous quarter to justify any score changes. Document score changes in the detection programme''s decisions.md (append-only).

Section Summary

What You Learned

Coverage measurement converts the technique inventory from a static list into a living programme metric. The three-tier scoring model (0/1/2) makes quality explicit: partial coverage is not full coverage and the score makes that visible to both engineers and leadership. Gap prioritisation converts the backlog from an unordered list into a ranked development plan ordered by likelihood × impact. The seven-track gateway scores six techniques at 1, three at 0, one intentional gap, and one deployment-specific partial — producing a clear roadmap: Chapter 12 closes four gaps to score 2, Chapter 13 closes two more, three red-cell gaps remain for future tracks. Coverage drift is caught by CI diff mode (weekly) and quarterly manual review for threat intelligence and taxonomy updates.

Key Terms

Term

Three-tier coverage score

Definition

The 0/1/2 scoring model for ATLAS technique coverage. 0 = no detection rule fires; 1 = at least one rule fires but confirmed evasion variants exist; 2 = all known variants covered, chain complete, human sign-off required for promotion.

Term

Coverage drift

Definition

The degradation of coverage scores over time due to rule regression via calibration, ATLAS taxonomy expansion, or attack variant evolution. Caught by scheduled diff runs and quarterly threat intelligence review.

Term

Gap prioritisation

Definition

The ranking of actionable coverage gaps by likelihood × impact score to produce an ordered detection development backlog. High-likelihood, high-impact gaps are built first.

Term

Coverage regression

Definition

A technique coverage score that decreases between two consecutive measurement runs. Caused by rule calibration that reduces true positive coverage, or by a new attack variant that evades all deployed rules. Triggers a CI failure in diff mode.

Term

Score-2 promotion

Definition

The manual act of advancing a technique from partial to full coverage. Requires chain_complete set to True in the ATLASTechnique registry and an explicit override passed to the coverage scorer. Cannot be auto-promoted by adding rules alone.
