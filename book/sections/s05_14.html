5.14.1

Title

NIST AI RMF Overview: Architecture and Rationale

Content

what_it_is: | NIST AI Risk Management Framework (NIST AI 100-1, January 2023) is a voluntary framework from the US National Institute of Standards and Technology for managing risks associated with artificial intelligence. It is non-prescriptive by design — it does not mandate specific controls or metrics, but provides a structured process for identifying, measuring, and managing AI risks throughout the system lifecycle. It is the primary AI risk standard referenced by: - US Executive Order 14110 (Section 4.1 directed NIST to extend AI standards; revoked January 2025) - EU AI Act Article 9 risk management requirements (structural alignment) - ISO/IEC 42001 AI Management System Standard (compatible) - Financial sector SR 11-7 model risk management guidance (complementary) why_engineers_need_to_understand_it: | NIST AI RMF is the language your CISO, board, and compliance team speak about AI risk. When the detection engineering programme from Part 3 needs budget, the justification lives in the MEASURE function. When you need organisational authority to gate a model deployment, the process lives in the GOVERN function. When an AI security incident occurs, the response process is defined by MANAGE. Engineers who understand only the technical language of ATLAS techniques and OWASP categories cannot communicate to the governance layer that approves budgets and sets policy. Engineers who understand both are the ones who build lasting programmes. four_function_architecture: function_govern: id: "GOVERN" purpose: "Establish policies, roles, accountability, and culture for AI risk management" temporal_position: "Before the AI system lifecycle begins — GOVERN is the foundation" key_question: "Who is responsible for what, and what are the rules?" primary_outputs: - "AI use policy (what AI may be used for, by whom, under what constraints)" - "Model governance procedure (how models are approved, deployed, and retired)" - "Role assignments (who owns AI risk, who approves deployments)" - "Accountability structure (escalation path for AI risk decisions)" - "Culture and training (AI risk awareness across the organisation)" function_map: id: "MAP" purpose: "Identify and categorise AI risks in context: who is affected, by what, how severely" temporal_position: "At system design and before each major deployment decision" key_question: "What are the risks of this specific AI system in this specific context?" primary_outputs: - "System context document (purpose, users, deployment environment, data flows)" - "Risk categorisation (using GOVERN-defined risk tiers)" - "Stakeholder impact inventory (who could be harmed, how, how severely)" - "Threat model (STRIDE or similar, incorporating ATLAS techniques)" - "Risk register entries (initial population from MAP)" function_measure: id: "MEASURE" purpose: "Analyse, test, evaluate, and monitor identified risks with quantitative and qualitative methods" temporal_position: "During development, at deployment gates, and continuously in production" key_question: "How bad are the risks, and how well are controls working?" primary_outputs: - "Bias audit results (Section 5.12 outputs)" - "Privacy risk audit results (Sections 5.10-5.11 outputs)" - "ATLAS detection programme coverage score (Section 5.8)" - "Model card with per-group metrics (Section 5.12.7)" - "MIA AUC and memorisation risk score (Sections 5.10-5.11)" - "Robustness benchmark results (adversarial test accuracy)" function_manage: id: "MANAGE" purpose: "Select and implement risk treatments; accept residual risk; plan incident response" temporal_position: "Following MEASURE; continuously as new risks emerge" key_question: "What are we going to do about the risks, and what residual risk do we accept?" primary_outputs: - "Risk treatment decisions (mitigate / accept / transfer / avoid)" - "Residual risk acceptance records (documented, authorised by appropriate level)" - "Incident response plan for AI-specific incidents" - "Risk register updates (treatments applied, residual risk stated)" - "Continuous improvement cycle (GOVERN → MAP → MEASURE → MANAGE → GOVERN)" sequential_logic: | The four functions are not independent — they form a dependency chain: GOVERN first: without policies and roles, MAP produces un-owned risks MAP second: without context, MEASURE measures the wrong things MEASURE third: without measurements, MANAGE makes uninformed treatment decisions MANAGE fourth: without treatment, risks remain at discovery-level severity In practice: GOVERN is established once and updated annually. MAP runs at system design and before major deployments. MEASURE runs continuously. MANAGE runs at defined decision gates and after measurement anomalies.

govern_rationale: | The GOVERN function exists because technical risk management without organisational authority fails. Title

MANAGE: Risk Treatment, Residual Risk Acceptance, and Incident Response

Content

manage_rationale: | MANAGE translates MEASURE outputs into decisions and actions. Without MANAGE, MEASURE produces metrics that inform nobody. The MANAGE function enforces three disciplines that are absent from most technical AI security programmes: (1) Explicit treatment decisions for every risk above appetite threshold (2) Documented residual risk acceptance — risk that was not mitigated is explicitly accepted, not implicitly ignored (3) Incident response planning before incidents occur, not during them four_risk_treatment_options: treatment_mitigate: definition: "Reduce probability and/or impact of the risk through controls" examples: - "Deploy ATLAS detection rule for AML.T0019 → mitigates supply chain risk" - "Apply DP training ε=4 → mitigates memorisation and MIA risk" - "Enforce SafeTensors format → mitigates pickle RCE supply chain risk" when_to_use: "Risk above risk appetite AND mitigation is technically feasible and cost-effective" documentation: "Control implemented (reference to technical implementation); residual risk after control" treatment_accept: definition: "Acknowledge the risk and accept it at its current level without additional controls" examples: - "AML.T0043 adversarial patches: not relevant to text-only deployment — accept" - "MIA AUC = 0.57 for general-purpose model: below threshold — accept" when_to_use: "Risk is below risk appetite threshold OR mitigation cost exceeds impact" documentation: "Explicit acceptance signed by appropriate authority; review date" treatment_transfer: definition: "Transfer the financial or operational impact to a third party" examples: - "Cyber insurance covering AI-related data breach costs" - "Contractual liability shift to model provider via BAA/DPA" when_to_use: "Risk cannot be fully mitigated but financial impact is insurable" documentation: "Insurance policy / contract reference; residual risk statement" treatment_avoid: definition: "Eliminate the risk by not undertaking the activity" examples: - "Do not fine-tune on PHI without DP training — avoid until DP is implemented" - "Do not deploy emotion recognition at workplace — prohibited by EU AI Act" when_to_use: "Risk is unacceptable and no feasible mitigation exists" documentation: "Decision record; alternative approach if applicable" residual_risk_acceptance_discipline: | Every risk in the MAP risk register must reach one of four states: MITIGATED: control deployed and MEASURE confirms effectiveness ACCEPTED: explicit acceptance documented with authority sign-off TRANSFERRED: financial risk transferred; operational risk documented AVOIDED: activity not undertaken Risks that are none of the above are "orphaned" — they exist in the register without a treatment decision. Orphaned risks are the most common source of actual AI security incidents. MANAGE enforces that every risk has a treatment. Residual risk acceptance record required fields: risk_id | current_risk_level | treatment_applied | residual_level | accepted_by | accepted_date | review_date ai_incident_response_plan_components: detection_triggers: - "ATLAS detection rule fires (any severity HIGH or above)" - "Canary token recall event in output scanner" - "MIA AUC exceeds threshold in monthly self-assessment" - "ATLAS coverage score drops below 0.70" - "Bias audit FNR ratio drops below 0.80 for any group" - "Supply chain artifact fails any of the six intake checks" response_procedure_phases: phase_01_contain: actions: ["Rate-limit or disable affected API key/endpoint", "Preserve logs for forensic analysis", "Notify AI Risk Owner within 1 hour"] phase_02_assess: actions: ["Determine scope: how many queries, what data exposed", "Classify incident severity using risk register", "Determine regulatory notification obligations (GDPR 72h, HIPAA, etc.)"] phase_03_remediate: actions: ["Apply technical fix (rotate keys, patch control, retrain with clean data)", "Verify fix effectiveness with MEASURE re-run", "Document root cause and contributing factors"] phase_04_recover: actions: ["Restore service with enhanced monitoring", "Update risk register with new entry for this incident type", "Feed incident into next GOVERN review cycle"] phase_05_review: actions: ["Post-incident review within 2 weeks", "Update MANAGE treatment for affected risk", "Share lessons learned with AI Risk Committee"] regulatory_notification_triggers: gdpr_72h: "Personal data breach from AI output — notify supervisory authority within 72h" eu_ai_act_serious_incident: "Serious incident or malfunction of high-risk AI — notify market surveillance authority" hipaa: "PHI breach from AI output — notify covered entity/OCR within 60 days" us_eo_14110: "Historical: reporting duty for qualifying models under EO 14110, revoked January 2025"

NIST AI RMF vs NIST Cybersecurity Framework: Differences and Integration

Content

csf_overview: | NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF 2.0) organises cybersecurity activities into six functions: GOVERN, IDENTIFY, PROTECT, DETECT, RESPOND, RECOVER. Most security teams are familiar with CSF. The AI RMF is not a replacement — it is a specialised overlay for AI-specific risks that the CSF was not designed to capture. three_key_differences: difference_01_scope_of_harm: csf: "Focuses on cybersecurity harms: CIA triad (confidentiality, integrity, availability)" ai_rmf: "Extends harm scope: includes fairness, explainability, privacy, safety — harms not reducible to CIA" implication: "AI-specific harms (discriminatory output, training data extraction, hallucination) require AI RMF categories beyond CSF's CIA framing" difference_02_lifecycle_coverage: csf: "Primarily addresses operational security — protects deployed systems" ai_rmf: "Covers full AI lifecycle: design decisions, training data, model development, deployment, and decommissioning" implication: "Supply chain risks (Section 5.9) and dataset poisoning (Section 5.9.3) occur before deployment — CSF PROTECT does not naturally cover them; AI RMF MAP does" difference_03_stakeholder_scope: csf: "Protects the organisation's systems and data" ai_rmf: "Explicitly protects all affected stakeholders: users, indirect subjects, third parties, and society" implication: "Bias affecting AI decisions on individuals is a NIST AI RMF risk; it is not naturally a CSF cybersecurity risk" two_integration_points: integration_01_detect_to_measure: csf_function: "DETECT" ai_rmf_function: "MEASURE (sub-function 2.4 — ongoing monitoring)" integration: | The CSF DETECT function (anomalies and events, continuous monitoring, detection processes) maps directly to NIST AI RMF MEASURE 2.4. The ATLAS Sigma rules from Section 5.8 are simultaneously CSF DETECT controls and AI RMF MEASURE 2.4 monitoring mechanisms. In a SIEM: tag ATLAS-derived alerts with both CSF.DE and AI_RMF.MEASURE.2.4 identifiers for dual-framework compliance reporting. integration_02_govern_to_govern: csf_function: "GOVERN (CSF 2.0)" ai_rmf_function: "GOVERN" integration: | Both frameworks have a GOVERN function as of CSF 2.0 (added in the 2024 update). CSF GOVERN establishes organisational cybersecurity risk strategy. AI RMF GOVERN establishes AI-specific risk policies within that strategy. An AI use policy should be an annex to or consistent with the organisation's CSF GOVERN cybersecurity policy — not a separate, potentially conflicting document. csf_to_ai_rmf_mapping_table: csf_govern: "AI RMF GOVERN — policies, roles, accountability (direct overlap)" csf_identify: "AI RMF MAP — risk identification, context, asset inventory (strong overlap)" csf_protect: "AI RMF MANAGE (mitigate) — control implementation (partial overlap)" csf_detect: "AI RMF MEASURE 2.4 — monitoring (strong overlap)" csf_respond: "AI RMF MANAGE (response planning) — incident response (partial overlap)" csf_recover: "AI RMF MANAGE (recovery) — restore and improve (partial overlap)" ai_rmf_unique: "MEASURE 2.1/2.2/2.3 (risk analysis, testing, evaluation) — not in CSF"

Implementation 01

Title

NIST AI RMF Mapping Template + Implementation Scorecard

Filename

nist_ai_rmf_tools.py

Purpose

Two tools: (1) RMFMappingTemplate: structured template for mapping an LLM deployment to all four NIST AI RMF functions, populating required outputs per function, and linking to specific Chapter 5 technical implementations. (2) RMFScorecard: maturity assessment across all four functions using a 0-4 maturity scale (absent → initial → defined → managed → optimising). Produces per-function maturity level, overall score, and prioritised improvement actions.

python

Four-function assessment with Chapter 5 implementation linkage.

@dataclass
class ScorecardEntry:
    function: str
    current_level: int
    target_level: int
    current_description: str
    gap_actions: List[str]


@dataclass
class RMFScorecard:
    model_id: str
    entries: List[ScorecardEntry]
    overall_score: float
    priority_actions: List[str]


def assess_maturity(model_id: str,
                    levels: Dict[str, int],
                    targets: Optional[Dict[str, int]] = None) -> RMFScorecard:
    targets = targets or {"GOVERN": 3, "MAP": 3, "MEASURE": 3, "MANAGE": 3}
    entries = []
    all_actions = []
    for fn in ["GOVERN", "MAP", "MEASURE", "MANAGE"]:
        lvl = levels.get(fn, 0)
        tgt = targets.get(fn, 3)
        gaps = []
        for next_lvl in range(lvl + 1, tgt + 1):
            gaps.append(f"Level {next_lvl}: {MATURITY_CRITERIA[fn][next_lvl]}")
        all_actions.extend(f"{fn}: {g}" for g in gaps[:1])
        entries.append(ScorecardEntry(
            function=fn, current_level=lvl, target_level=tgt,
            current_description=MATURITY_CRITERIA[fn][lvl],
            gap_actions=gaps,
        ))
    overall = sum(e.current_level for e in entries) / (4 * 4) * 100
    return RMFScorecard(model_id=model_id, entries=entries,
                         overall_score=round(overall, 1),
                         priority_actions=all_actions[:4])


def print_scorecard(s: RMFScorecard) -> None:
    print(f"\n{'='*60}")
    print(f"NIST AI RMF MATURITY SCORECARD: {s.model_id}")
    print(f"Overall maturity: {s.overall_score:.1f}% (max 100%)")
    print(f"\n{'Function':10s}  {'Current':>8}  {'Target':>8}  {'Gap':>6}")
    print("-" * 40)
    for e in s.entries:
        gap = e.target_level - e.current_level
        print(f"{e.function:10s}  {e.current_level:>6}/4  {e.target_level:>6}/4  "
              f"{'×' * gap if gap else 'OK':>6}")
        print(f"           {e.current_description[:55]}")
    if s.priority_actions:
        print(f"\nTop priority actions:")
        for a in s.priority_actions:
            print(f"  → {a[:70]}")

"""
MEASURE Metrics Dashboard + Risk Register Integration
RAG status, trend tracking, risk register with treatment decisions.
"""

from dataclasses import dataclass, field
from datetime import date
from typing import Dict, List, Optional


# -----------------------------------------------------------------------
# Metrics Dashboard
# -----------------------------------------------------------------------

@dataclass
class Metric:
    name: str
    value: float
    unit: str
    target: float
    direction: str       # "lower_is_better" / "higher_is_better"
    previous_value: Optional[float]
    source_tool: str
    chapter5_section: str
    review_cadence: str

    @property
    def rag(self) -> str:
        if self.direction == "higher_is_better":
            ratio = self.value / max(self.target, 1e-9)
        else:
            ratio = self.target / max(self.value, 1e-9)
        return "GREEN" if ratio >= 1.0 else "AMBER" if ratio >= 0.75 else "RED"

    @property
    def trend(self) -> str:
        if self.previous_value is None:
            return "NEW"
        if self.direction == "higher_is_better":
            return "UP" if self.value > self.previous_value else "DOWN" if self.value < self.previous_value else "FLAT"
        return "DOWN" if self.value < self.previous_value else "UP" if self.value > self.previous_value else "FLAT"

    @property
    def trend_is_good(self) -> bool:
        if self.trend == "NEW":
            return True
        if self.direction == "higher_is_better":
            return self.trend == "UP"
        return self.trend == "DOWN"


class MetricsDashboard:
    def __init__(self, model_id: str):
        self.model_id = model_id
        self.metrics: List[Metric] = []

    def add(self, m: Metric) -> None:
        self.metrics.append(m)

    def posture_score(self) -> float:
        """Fraction of metrics at GREEN."""
        if not self.metrics:
            return 0.0
        return round(sum(1 for m in self.metrics if m.rag == "GREEN") / len(self.metrics) * 100, 1)

    def critical_reds(self) -> List[Metric]:
        return [m for m in self.metrics if m.rag == "RED"]

    def print_dashboard(self) -> None:
        rag_icons = {"GREEN": "GREEN", "AMBER": "AMBER", "RED": "RED  "}
        trend_icons = {"UP": "↑", "DOWN": "↓", "FLAT": "→", "NEW": "·"}
        print(f"\n{'='*70}")
        print(f"NIST AI RMF MEASURE DASHBOARD: {self.model_id}")
        print(f"Security Posture Score: {self.posture_score():.1f}%  |  "
              f"Metrics: {len(self.metrics)}  |  "
              f"RED: {len(self.critical_reds())}")
        print(f"\n{'Metric':35s}  {'Value':>10}  {'Target':>8}  {'Status':>7}  {'Trend':>5}")
        print("-" * 70)
        for m in sorted(self.metrics, key=lambda x: (x.rag != "RED", x.rag != "AMBER")):
            trend_sym = trend_icons.get(m.trend, "?")
            trend_color = "✓" if m.trend_is_good else "!"
            print(f"{m.name[:35]:35s}  {m.value:>9.3f}{m.unit[0]:1}  "
                  f"{m.target:>8.3f}  [{rag_icons[m.rag]}]  "
                  f"{trend_sym}{trend_color}")
        if self.critical_reds():
            print(f"\nRED METRICS — immediate action required:")
            for m in self.critical_reds():
                print(f"  {m.name}: {m.value:.3f} (target: {m.target:.3f}) — {m.source_tool}")


# -----------------------------------------------------------------------
# Risk Register
# -----------------------------------------------------------------------

@dataclass
class RiskEntry:
    risk_id: str
    category: str
    description: str
    atlas_techniques: List[str]
    likelihood: str       # "low" / "medium" / "high"
    impact: str           # "low" / "medium" / "high" / "critical"
    treatment: str        # "mitigate" / "accept" / "transfer" / "avoid"
    control_ref: str
    residual_likelihood: str
    residual_impact: str
    accepted_by: str
    accepted_date: str
    review_date: str
    status: str           # "open" / "mitigated" / "accepted" / "closed"


LIKELIHOOD_RANK = {"low": 1, "medium": 2, "high": 3}
IMPACT_RANK = {"low": 1, "medium": 2, "high": 3, "critical": 4}


class RiskRegister:
    def __init__(self, model_id: str):
        self.model_id = model_id
        self.entries: List[RiskEntry] = []

    def add(self, entry: RiskEntry) -> None:
        self.entries.append(entry)

    def open_risks(self) -> List[RiskEntry]:
        return [e for e in self.entries if e.status == "open"]

    def orphaned_risks(self) -> List[RiskEntry]:
        """Risks with no treatment decision."""
        return [e for e in self.entries if e.treatment == ""]

    def priority_sort(self) -> List[RiskEntry]:
        return sorted(self.entries,
                      key=lambda e: -(LIKELIHOOD_RANK.get(e.residual_likelihood, 0) *
                                      IMPACT_RANK.get(e.residual_impact, 0)))

    def print_register(self) -> None:
        print(f"\n{'='*70}")
        print(f"RISK REGISTER: {self.model_id}  ({len(self.entries)} risks)")
        orphaned = self.orphaned_risks()
        if orphaned:
            print(f"WARNING: {len(orphaned)} orphaned risks (no treatment decision)")
        print(f"\n{'ID':>6}  {'Category':20s}  {'Treatment':10s}  "
              f"{'Residual L':>10}  {'Residual I':>10}  {'Status':>10}")
        print("-" * 70)
        for e in self.priority_sort():
            print(f"{e.risk_id:>6}  {e.category[:20]:20s}  {e.treatment[:10]:10s}  "
                  f"{e.residual_likelihood:>10}  {e.residual_impact:>10}  {e.status:>10}")

Key Takeaways

Takeaway 01

NIST AI RMF is the management language for technical AI security work. The GOVERN function provides organisational authority to gate deployments. The MAP function scopes what risks to measure. The MEASURE function is where the technical implementations from Sections 5.8-5.12 live. The MANAGE function ensures every measured risk reaches a documented treatment decision. Engineers who understand all four functions can translate security work into budget requests and governance decisions.

Takeaway 02

GOVERN must precede MAP, MAP must precede MEASURE, and MEASURE must precede MANAGE. This is not bureaucracy — it is dependency management. A detection programme without deployment authority (GOVERN) generates alerts nobody acts on. A bias audit without a deployment gate (GOVERN gate 4) produces findings nobody blocks. Sequence matters.

Takeaway 03

The MEASURE sub-function 2.4 (ongoing monitoring) is the production detection engineering programme. Every Sigma rule from Section 5.8, every output scanner from Section 5.10-5.11, and every bias drift monitor from Section 5.12 is a NIST AI RMF MEASURE 2.4 control. Tagging these controls with MEASURE sub-function identifiers in the SIEM produces regulatory compliance evidence as a by-product of normal security operations.

Takeaway 04

Orphaned risks are the primary source of AI security incidents. A risk that appears in the MAP risk register but has no documented treatment decision is neither mitigated nor accepted — it simply exists. The MANAGE function's discipline of requiring every risk to reach a treatment state (mitigate / accept / transfer / avoid) closes this gap. The risk register implementation enforces this by flagging orphaned risks explicitly.

Takeaway 05

Residual risk acceptance is a governance act, not a technical one. The statement "we accept the residual risk of MIA AUC = 0.61 against our general-purpose assistant because it is within the 0.70 threshold for this data type" must be signed by the AI Risk Owner, not decided by the detection engineer. Documenting this signature is what transforms a technical measurement into a defensible governance decision.

Takeaway 06

NIST AI RMF and NIST CSF are complementary, not competitive. The three unique AI RMF sub-functions (MEASURE 2.1 risk analysis, 2.2 testing, 2.3 evaluation) have no CSF equivalent. The two overlap points (GOVERN↔GOVERN, DETECT↔MEASURE 2.4) are integration points, not duplications. Organisations that run both frameworks should tag controls with both identifiers and produce a single unified evidence package per control.

Takeaway 07

The model governance procedure with six lifecycle gates is the operationalisation of NIST AI RMF GOVERN. Gate 4 (pre-deployment) is the most consequential: it is the point where MEASURE outputs (bias audit, privacy audit, ATLAS coverage, ML SBOM) are reviewed and a deployment decision is made. This is where the technical work of Chapters 5-17 produces its most direct business value — either a confident deployment decision with documented evidence, or a blocked deployment that prevents a future incident.

Takeaway 08

The bridge to Part 3 is direct. Chapters 11-17 operationalise NIST AI RMF MEASURE sub-functions 2.1 through 2.4 and MANAGE at production scale. Engineers building the detection engineering programme in Part 3 are simultaneously implementing the NIST AI RMF MEASURE and MANAGE functions and producing compliance evidence for EU AI Act Article 15 and GDPR Article 25, and the evidence US federal guidance has asked for (EO 14110 until its revocation in January 2025). The technical work and the governance framework are not separate activities — they are the same work described in two languages.

Section Summary

One Paragraph

Section 5.14 establishes NIST AI RMF as the organisational scaffolding that makes the technical security work of Sections 5.8-5.13 consequential — connecting technical measurements to governance decisions, budget approvals, and deployment authorities. The four-function architecture is presented with its sequential dependency logic: GOVERN (policies and roles before any other function), MAP (risk identification and context before measurement), MEASURE (the technical engine — all implementations from Sections 5.8-5.12 live here), and MANAGE (treatment decisions and residual risk acceptance for every identified risk). The GOVERN function is operationalised through an AI use policy template, a six-gate model governance procedure, role assignment definitions, and a four-level maturity taxonomy from absent to optimising. The MAP function produces three artefacts — system context document, stakeholder impact inventory, and initial risk register — with the NIST AI 100-1 trustworthiness categories mapped to specific ATLAS techniques. The MEASURE function is the most technically dense: a 12-metric catalog covers security (ATLAS coverage score, supply chain pass rate, extraction alerts), privacy (memorisation risk score, MIA AUC, canary recall events), fairness (max FNR ratio, exploitability score, sentiment disparity), and compliance (EU AI Act compliance percentage, GDPR article gap count), each with target threshold, source tool, and review cadence. The MANAGE function establishes four treatment options (mitigate, accept, transfer, avoid), the residual risk acceptance discipline (every risk must reach a treatment state — no orphans), and a five-phase AI incident response plan with regulatory notification triggers. NIST AI RMF and NIST CSF are compared across three dimensions of difference (harm scope, lifecycle coverage, stakeholder scope) and two integration points (DETECT↔MEASURE 2.4 and GOVERN↔GOVERN). The section closes with a full RAG deployment worked example and explicit bridge mapping: the Part 3 detection engineering programme (Chapters 11-17) is the production operationalisation of NIST AI RMF MEASURE and MANAGE. Three implementations deliver the RMF mapping template with 27 required outputs and the maturity scorecard, the GOVERN policy template generator producing AI use policy and model governance procedure in Markdown, and the MEASURE metrics dashboard with RAG status tracking paired with the MANAGE risk register.
