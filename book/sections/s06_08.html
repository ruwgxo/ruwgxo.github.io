Subsection 1

Number

6.8.1

Title

Why Email Injection Is Uniquely Dangerous

Content

Email injection occupies a distinct position among indirect injection attack classes because the adversary can deliver the injection vector directly to the target organisation without any access to the target's systems. Every other indirect injection surface requires the adversary to first compromise or influence an external data source -- a knowledge base document, a web page, a database record. Email requires none of that. The adversary sends an email. An LLM email assistant is a deployment that processes incoming emails on behalf of a user or organisation: summarising, categorising, drafting replies, or taking actions based on email content. The email body is retrieved from the mail server and inserted into the LLM context as content to be processed. Any email the LLM assistant processes is a potential injection vector. Any external sender who can reach the organisation's mail server has indirect injection capability. The threat model is fundamentally different from RAG or web injection. In RAG injection, the adversary must influence content that is indexed and retrieved. In web injection, the adversary must own or compromise a page the agent browses. In email injection, the adversary sends an email. The barrier to exploitation is the lowest of any indirect injection surface. The consequence of this low barrier is that email injection has the largest potential adversary population of any indirect injection class. Spam filters and email authentication (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) reduce but do not eliminate adversary email delivery. Adversaries who can bypass spam filters -- through legitimate-looking email content, compromised sender accounts, or newly registered domains -- can deliver injection payloads to any LLM email assistant that processes their incoming mail. A second property that makes email injection especially dangerous is that LLM email assistants frequently have send capability. An assistant that can read and summarise emails is useful; an assistant that can also draft and send replies is more useful. The send capability creates a direct confused deputy attack surface: injection payload in an incoming email causes the assistant to send a reply -- with sensitive context, with a phishing attachment, or to an adversary-controlled address -- using the organisation's email identity and the assistant's legitimate permissions.

Key Point

Email injection has the lowest adversary barrier of any indirect injection surface: the attack vector is sent directly to the target. LLM email assistants with send capability are confused deputy attacks waiting for an injection trigger. No access to target systems is required.

6.8.2

Title

Email Body, Subject Line, and HTML Email Injection

Content

Email body injection is the baseline case. An adversary crafts an email whose body contains an injection payload alongside legitimate-appearing content. When the LLM email assistant processes the email -- to summarise it, categorise it, or draft a reply -- the injection payload enters the LLM context as content of an incoming message. The assistant has no mechanism to distinguish injection instructions within email content from legitimate email content, because email content is by design user-controlled natural language. The effective email body injection embeds the payload in content that is semantically appropriate for the email's apparent purpose. An adversary who wants to trigger the injection when the assistant processes what appears to be a vendor invoice embeds the payload within invoice-like content, so that the email passes automated email filtering heuristics and appears legitimate to any human who might spot-check the assistant's output. Subject line injection: the email subject is a short, high-visibility field that LLM email assistants frequently use as a summary or categorisation anchor. A payload in the subject line is processed early in the email handling context and may have disproportionate influence relative to its length. Subject line injection is limited by character count constraints but effective for short, high-impact instructions -- particularly for directing the assistant's immediate behaviour rather than delivering a complex payload. HTML email injection replicates the web injection techniques from Section 6.7 in the email context. HTML emails are rendered by mail clients, and the rendering suppresses the same categories of hidden content as web browsers: CSS display:none elements, HTML comment content, meta tag content, and off-screen text. An LLM email assistant that processes the email's HTML source or extracts text from HTML using a naive extractor will include all hidden content. The same CSS stripping and content normalisation pipeline from Section 6.7 must be applied to HTML email content before injection classification. The HTML email injection surface is particularly relevant because HTML email is the dominant format for commercial and marketing communications -- the category of email that LLM email assistants are most commonly used to process.

Key Point

Email body and subject line injection are direct entry points requiring only email delivery access. HTML email injection replicates CSS hidden content techniques from web injection inside the email format. The Section 6.7 normalisation pipeline applies without modification.

Title

Reply Chain and Forwarded Email Injection

Content

Reply chain injection exploits the structure of email conversation threads. When an email assistant processes a reply or a forwarded thread, the full conversation history -- including all prior messages in the thread -- is typically included in the email body as quoted text. An adversary who injects a payload into an early message in a thread, or into a message that gets forwarded through a chain, can have that payload processed when a later legitimate reply is handled by the assistant. The attack structure: an adversary sends an initial email to a target contact, not to the organisation using the LLM assistant. The initial email appears legitimate. The contact replies, with the adversary's email as quoted text at the bottom of the reply. The contact may forward the thread to the organisation. When the LLM email assistant processes the forwarded thread or reply, it processes the full thread content including the adversary's original email at the bottom of the quoted section. The injection payload, buried in quoted thread history, enters the LLM context. The trusted sender bypass is the most operationally significant property of this attack. The email received by the LLM assistant was sent by a trusted contact -- a colleague, a known vendor, a regular correspondent. The email appears to originate from a trusted source. The injection is in the quoted content within the email body, attributed to the adversary, but the assistant may not apply differential trust levels to quoted versus non-quoted sections. Forwarded email injection uses the same mechanism via a different path. An adversary sends a carefully crafted email to a member of the target organisation -- anyone who might forward email to someone whose email is processed by the LLM assistant. When the recipient forwards the adversary's email, the full adversary-controlled content is forwarded intact as the email body. The forwarded email arrives in the LLM assistant's processing queue attributed to a trusted internal forwarder. Detection signal: the assistant should apply injection classification to all sections of the email body, including quoted thread history and forwarded content, not only the most recent message. Quoted content should be extracted and classified separately, with a flag indicating that the content originates from a potentially untrusted prior correspondent.

Key Point

Reply chain and forwarded email injection bypass trusted sender heuristics by embedding payloads in quoted or forwarded content attributed to external correspondents. Detection must classify all sections of the email body, including quoted history, not only the most recent message.

Title

Email-Triggered Action Escalation: The Confused Deputy Scenario

Content

Email-triggered action escalation is the highest-impact email injection outcome. It occurs when an LLM email assistant has outbound capabilities -- the ability to send emails, create calendar events, update CRM records, or call external APIs -- and an injection payload directs the assistant to use those capabilities for adversary purposes. The canonical scenario: an adversary sends an email to a target organisation whose LLM email assistant automatically handles incoming correspondence. The email contains a legitimate-appearing body and an injection payload. The payload instructs the assistant to reply to the email including the contents of the system prompt or recent conversation context. The assistant, following the injection instruction, sends a reply to the adversary containing sensitive information. The adversary receives the reply without ever accessing the target's systems. The entire attack is conducted through the email channel. Variations of this scenario exploit different capabilities the email assistant may have. If the assistant can create calendar invites, the injection may direct it to create a meeting that includes sensitive information in the description or that links to an adversary-controlled URL. If the assistant updates CRM records, the injection may direct it to write adversary-controlled content to a customer record that will subsequently be retrieved and processed by another LLM (creating a cross-system injection chain). If the assistant can forward emails, the injection may direct it to forward internal emails to an external address. The confused deputy property: the email assistant has legitimate access to the email send capability, the calendar, and the CRM. The adversary does not. Through the injection, the adversary uses the assistant's permissions to achieve what they could not achieve directly. The assistant is the deputy; the adversary's instructions are the confusion that redirects the deputy's legitimate access. The mitigation is the same principle applied in Section 6.7 sandboxing: the assistant's outbound capabilities must be explicitly enumerated and restricted. Automatic reply drafting is acceptable; automatic reply sending without human review is not. Action restriction at the tool permission layer is more reliable than attempting to detect and prevent all possible injection payloads that could trigger action escalation.

Key Point

LLM email assistants with send capability are confused deputy risks. An injection that causes the assistant to reply, forward, or take external action achieves adversary objectives using the assistant's legitimate permissions. Action restriction -- requiring human review before send -- is the primary mitigation.

6.8.5

Title

Calendar Invite Injection and Extended Integration Surface

Content

Calendar integration extends the email injection surface in two directions. An LLM assistant that processes calendar invites alongside email can receive injection payloads through calendar invite fields. An LLM assistant that creates calendar events based on email content can be directed by email injection to create calendar events with adversary-specified content. Calendar invite injection: calendar invites (iCalendar format, .ics files) contain multiple text fields: summary (the event title), description (free-form event details), location (physical or virtual location), attendee fields, and comments. Each text field is a potential injection vector. An adversary who sends a calendar invite to a target whose LLM assistant processes calendar invites can embed injection payloads in the description or comment fields. The invite summary ("Meeting re: Q3 Budget Review") appears legitimate; the injection is in the description field that the user may not read before accepting. The description field in particular has high injection potential because it is free-form text of arbitrary length, the assistant may read it in full to provide context for meeting preparation, and it is not visible in most calendar UI summary views (requiring the user to click into the event to see it). An injection payload in a calendar invite description is therefore less likely to be spotted by human review than a payload in an email body. Integration surface expansion: when email assistants are integrated with task management tools, CRM systems, or document management platforms -- common in enterprise deployments -- each integration point adds an additional confused deputy surface. An injection that directs the assistant to create a task in a connected project management tool, write a note in a CRM record, or store a document in a connected drive extends the injection's impact beyond the email channel into the broader enterprise toolchain. Detection: calendar invite text fields must be classified alongside email body content. The same injection classifier applies; the normalisation pipeline must handle iCalendar text encoding (quoted-printable, base64, and line-folding artefacts). Integration surface expansion must be assessed as part of the email assistant deployment threat model, with each integration treated as an additional confused deputy attack surface.

Key Point

Calendar invite description fields are high-capacity, low-visibility injection vectors. Integration of the email assistant with CRM, task management, or document tools extends each injection's potential impact into the connected toolchain. Each integration adds a confused deputy surface.

Title

Email Content Normalisation Pipeline

Content

The email content normalisation pipeline adapts the Section 6.7 web normalisation pipeline to the email format, with email-specific extensions for MIME structure, quoted content extraction, and email header classification. Stage A -- MIME structure parsing: email messages use MIME (Multipurpose Internet Mail Extensions) to carry multipart content. A single email may contain a plain-text part, an HTML part, attachments, and inline images. The normalisation pipeline must parse the MIME structure and process each content part appropriately. The HTML part requires full CSS stripping as in Section 6.7. The plain-text part is processed directly. Attachments are classified separately (file type detection, content extraction where applicable). Stage B -- HTML email normalisation: apply the Section 6.7 six-stage pipeline (encoding normalisation, script and style removal, non-rendered element removal, meta content extraction, body text extraction, classification) to the HTML MIME part. This handles CSS-hidden injection in HTML emails with no additional specialisation required. Stage C -- Quoted content extraction and segmentation: email body text contains quoted prior messages, typically prefixed with ">" characters or interspersed with "On [date], [sender] wrote:" separators. Extract each quoted segment as a separate text block, annotating it with its depth in the reply chain (depth 1 = most recent quoted message, depth 2 = the message quoted within that message, etc.). Apply injection classification to each segment independently, with the quoted-depth annotation influencing the risk weight (deeper quoted content from potentially unknown correspondents carries higher injection suspicion). Stage D -- Header field classification: email headers contain metadata that the LLM assistant may include in its processing context: subject, sender display name, reply-to address, X-custom headers. Injection payloads can appear in any header field that the assistant reads. The subject field requires injection classification. Sender display name fields ("Alice Smith <alice@example.com>") can contain arbitrary text in the display portion and should be classified. X-custom headers with content processed by the assistant must be classified. Stage E -- iCalendar content parsing: for calendar invites, extract all text-bearing fields (SUMMARY, DESCRIPTION, LOCATION, COMMENT, X-custom properties) and classify each. Apply quoted-printable and base64 decoding as appropriate for the field encoding. Stage F -- Normalised output assembly and classification routing: combine all classified segments into a structured result indicating which segments are clean, which are flagged, and which are blocked. The LLM context assembly stage receives only the clean segments, with flagged segments routed to review.

Key Point

Email normalisation extends the Section 6.7 pipeline with MIME structure parsing, quoted content segmentation with depth annotation, header field classification, and iCalendar field extraction. Quoted content from external correspondents carries elevated injection suspicion proportional to reply chain depth.

6.8.7

Title

Email Agent Sandboxing and Detection Architecture

Content

The email agent sandboxing architecture applies the same three-component model from Section 6.7 -- action restriction, network egress control, and session isolation -- with email-specific adaptations. Action restriction for email agents: the primary high-risk capability is outbound email sending. The sandboxing policy should require human review and approval before any outbound email is sent by the assistant. Draft-and- hold is the safe default: the assistant prepares a draft, which is queued for human review before sending. Automatic sending should be restricted to a defined set of pre-approved recipient addresses and content templates, with any deviation from the template requiring human approval. Secondary action restrictions: calendar event creation should require human confirmation before the event is published to external attendees. CRM record updates triggered by email processing should be staged in a review queue before committing. Forwarding to external addresses should require explicit human authorisation for each forward. Detection signals specific to email injection: email injection produces several signals that do not appear in web or RAG injection. The injection payload is delivered in a single email event that is logged with a timestamp and sender attribution. This means email injection leaves a more attributable trace than web or RAG injection -- the email is logged, the sender address is recorded, and the delivery timestamp is known. Post-incident investigation of email injection is therefore more tractable than investigation of RAG injection where the poisoned document may have been in the knowledge base for weeks. The pre-processing classifier placement for email is immediately after MIME parsing and before any content is delivered to the LLM context. Each email segment (body text, HTML content, quoted sections, header fields, calendar fields) is independently classified. The classification result determines whether the email is processed normally, flagged for review, or blocked from reaching the LLM entirely. Output monitoring for email agents monitors the assistant's draft outputs for signs of injection-influenced behaviour: unusual recipients in reply-to fields, system prompt content in the draft body, abnormal attachment requests, or draft content that is inconsistent with the email subject and body context. Output monitoring is the backstop when the pre-processing classifier misses the payload.

Key Point

Email agent sandboxing centres on draft-and-hold: the assistant prepares outbound emails for human review before sending. This single control prevents the most impactful email injection outcome -- data exfiltration via email reply -- regardless of whether the input classifier catches the injection payload.

#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""
email_injection_classifier.py
Section 6.8 -- Email Content Normalisation and Injection Classifier
Parses MIME email structure, normalises all content segments, classifies
each for injection signals, and produces a consolidated email-level decision.
Author: Raghav Dinesh | ai-security-mastery
"""

import base64
import email as email_lib
import html as html_mod
import json
import quopri
import re
import unicodedata
from dataclasses import dataclass, field, asdict
from email import policy
from email.message import Message
from pathlib import Path
from typing import Optional


# ---- Injection signal patterns (email-aware extensions) -------------

INJECTION_SIGNALS = [
    r"\bignore\b.{0,40}\b(previous|prior|all)\b.{0,20}\b(instruction|directive|context)\b",
    r"\b(forget|disregard|override)\b.{0,30}\b(instruction|told|above)\b",
    r"\byour (new|actual|real) (directive|task|role|instruction)\b",
    r"\bnote to (ai|assistant|system|llm)\b",
    r"\b(output|reveal|repeat|show|include)\b.{0,30}\b(system prompt|configuration|instructions?)\b",
    r"\bask.{0,30}(user|them|recipient).{0,30}(password|credential|token|key)\b",
    r"\b(reply|respond|send|forward).{0,30}(to|this|email).{0,30}(adversary|attacker|external)\b",
    r"\b(attach|include|send).{0,30}(confidential|sensitive|internal|private)\b",
    r"\bforward.{0,20}(this|all|these).{0,20}(email|message|thread)\b.{0,20}(to|at)\b",
    r"\bno (content |safety )?restrict",
    r"\byou are now\b",
    r"\bimportant.{0,10}(system|update|directive|override)\b",
]

ZERO_WIDTH = {"\u200B", "\u200C", "\u200D", "\uFEFF"}


def score_injection(text: str) -> float:
    if not text:
        return 0.0
    text_l = text.lower()
    matches = sum(
        1 for p in INJECTION_SIGNALS
        if re.search(p, text_l, re.IGNORECASE)
    )
    return min(1.0, matches * 0.22)


def strip_css_hidden(html_text: str) -> str:
    """Strip CSS-hidden elements and HTML comments from email HTML."""
    # Remove script and style blocks
    text = re.sub(r"<script[^>]*>.*?</script>", " ", html_text, flags=re.DOTALL | re.IGNORECASE)
    text = re.sub(r"<style[^>]*>.*?</style>", " ", text, flags=re.DOTALL | re.IGNORECASE)
    # Remove HTML comments
    text = re.sub(r"<!--.*?-->", " ", text, flags=re.DOTALL)
    # Remove display:none and visibility:hidden elements (inline style)
    text = re.sub(
        r"<[^>]+style=['\"][^'\"]*(?:display\s*:\s*none|visibility\s*:\s*hidden|opacity\s*:\s*0)[^'\"]*['\"][^>]*>.*?</[^>]+>",
        " ", text, flags=re.DOTALL | re.IGNORECASE
    )
    # Strip remaining tags
    text = re.sub(r"<[^>]+>", " ", text)
    # Decode HTML entities
    text = html_mod.unescape(text)
    return re.sub(r"\s{2,}", " ", text).strip()


def decode_mime_payload(part: Message) -> str:
    """Decode a MIME part payload to a string."""
    charset = part.get_content_charset() or "utf-8"
    payload = part.get_payload(decode=True)
    if payload is None:
        return ""
    try:
        return payload.decode(charset, errors="replace")
    except Exception:
        return payload.decode("utf-8", errors="replace")


def extract_quoted_segments(plain_text: str) -> list[tuple[int, str]]:
    """
    Extract quoted reply segments from plain text email body.
    Returns list of (depth, text) tuples. depth=0 is the main body,
    depth=1 is the first level of quoted content, etc.
    """
    segments = []
    current_depth = 0
    current_lines: list[str] = []

    for line in plain_text.splitlines():
        stripped = line.lstrip()
        depth = len(line) - len(stripped)
        # Count leading '>' characters as quote depth
        gt_depth = 0
        temp = stripped
        while temp.startswith(">"):
            gt_depth += 1
            temp = temp[1:].lstrip()

        if gt_depth != current_depth:
            if current_lines:
                segments.append((current_depth, "\n".join(current_lines)))
            current_lines = [temp if gt_depth > 0 else stripped]
            current_depth = gt_depth
        else:
            current_lines.append(temp if gt_depth > 0 else stripped)

    if current_lines:
        segments.append((current_depth, "\n".join(current_lines)))

    return segments


def normalise_text(text: str) -> str:
    """Remove zero-width chars, NFKC normalise, collapse whitespace."""
    text = "".join(c for c in text if c not in ZERO_WIDTH)
    text = unicodedata.normalize("NFKC", text)
    return re.sub(r"\s{2,}", " ", text).strip()


@dataclass
class EmailSegmentResult:
    segment_type: str       # body | html_body | quoted_depth_N | header_subject |
                            # header_sender | header_reply_to | calendar_field
    content_preview: str
    injection_score: float
    matched_signals: list[str]
    decision: str           # ALLOW | FLAG | BLOCK


@dataclass
class EmailClassificationResult:
    message_id: str
    sender: str
    subject: str
    segment_results: list[EmailSegmentResult]
    email_injection_score: float
    email_decision: str     # ALLOW | FLAG | BLOCK
    action_escalation_risk: bool
    audit_notes: list[str]


def classify_segment(
    segment_type: str,
    text: str,
    flag_threshold: float = 0.30,
    block_threshold: float = 0.60,
    depth_weight: float = 1.0,
) -> EmailSegmentResult:
    normalised = normalise_text(text)
    score = score_injection(normalised) * depth_weight

    matched = [
        p[:60] for p in INJECTION_SIGNALS
        if re.search(p, normalised.lower(), re.IGNORECASE)
    ]

    if score >= block_threshold:
        decision = "BLOCK"
    elif score >= flag_threshold:
        decision = "FLAG"
    else:
        decision = "ALLOW"

    return EmailSegmentResult(
        segment_type=segment_type,
        content_preview=text[:80],
        injection_score=round(score, 4),
        matched_signals=matched[:3],
        decision=decision,
    )


def check_action_escalation_risk(segments: list[EmailSegmentResult]) -> bool:
    """
    Check if any segment contains signals that would trigger
    action escalation (email send, forward, CRM write, etc).
    """
    escalation_patterns = [
        r"\b(reply|send|forward|email).{0,20}(to|this|at)\b",
        r"\b(attach|include)\b.{0,20}(confidential|internal|private|sensitive)\b",
        r"\bcreate.{0,20}(calendar|event|meeting|invite)\b",
        r"\b(update|write|record|save).{0,20}(crm|database|record|note)\b",
    ]
    for seg in segments:
        for pat in escalation_patterns:
            if re.search(pat, seg.content_preview.lower(), re.IGNORECASE):
                return True
    return False


def classify_email(
    raw_email: str,
    flag_threshold: float = 0.30,
    block_threshold: float = 0.60,
) -> EmailClassificationResult:
    msg = email_lib.message_from_string(raw_email, policy=policy.default)
    segment_results = []
    audit_notes = []

    sender = str(msg.get("From", ""))
    subject = str(msg.get("Subject", ""))
    message_id = str(msg.get("Message-ID", "unknown"))
    reply_to = str(msg.get("Reply-To", ""))

    # Stage D: header classification
    if subject:
        seg = classify_segment("header_subject", subject, flag_threshold, block_threshold)
        segment_results.append(seg)

    if sender:
        # Extract display name only (the part before <email@addr>)
        display_name = re.sub(r"<[^>]+>", "", sender).strip()
        if display_name:
            seg = classify_segment("header_sender_display", display_name,
                                   flag_threshold, block_threshold)
            segment_results.append(seg)

    if reply_to:
        seg = classify_segment("header_reply_to", reply_to,
                               flag_threshold, block_threshold)
        segment_results.append(seg)

    # Walk MIME parts
    for part in msg.walk():
        content_type = part.get_content_type()
        disposition = part.get_content_disposition() or ""

        if "attachment" in disposition:
            audit_notes.append(f"Attachment detected: {part.get_filename()}")
            continue

        if content_type == "text/plain":
            body_text = decode_mime_payload(part)
            quoted_segments = extract_quoted_segments(body_text)

            for depth, seg_text in quoted_segments:
                seg_type = f"body" if depth == 0 else f"quoted_depth_{depth}"
                # Quoted content from external correspondents gets higher weight
                depth_weight = 1.0 if depth == 0 else min(2.0, 1.0 + depth * 0.3)
                seg_result = classify_segment(
                    seg_type, seg_text, flag_threshold, block_threshold,
                    depth_weight=depth_weight
                )
                segment_results.append(seg_result)

        elif content_type == "text/html":
            html_body = decode_mime_payload(part)
            visible_text = strip_css_hidden(html_body)
            seg_result = classify_segment(
                "html_body", visible_text, flag_threshold, block_threshold
            )
            segment_results.append(seg_result)

            # Also check raw HTML for hidden content injection
            all_text = re.sub(r"<[^>]+>", " ", html_body)
            all_text_clean = normalise_text(html_mod.unescape(all_text))
            if score_injection(all_text_clean) > score_injection(visible_text):
                audit_notes.append("Hidden content detected: raw HTML score > visible text score")
                hidden_seg = classify_segment(
                    "html_hidden_content", all_text_clean,
                    flag_threshold, block_threshold
                )
                segment_results.append(hidden_seg)

        elif content_type == "text/calendar":
            cal_text = decode_mime_payload(part)
            for cal_field in ("SUMMARY", "DESCRIPTION", "LOCATION", "COMMENT"):
                pattern = re.compile(
                    rf"^{cal_field}[;:](.*)$", re.MULTILINE | re.IGNORECASE
                )
                for m in pattern.finditer(cal_text):
                    field_val = m.group(1).strip()
                    if field_val:
                        seg_result = classify_segment(
                            f"calendar_{cal_field.lower()}",
                            field_val, flag_threshold, block_threshold
                        )
                        segment_results.append(seg_result)

    if not segment_results:
        audit_notes.append("No classifiable content found in email")

    # Consolidated decision: worst single segment determines email decision
    max_score = max((s.injection_score for s in segment_results), default=0.0)

    if max_score >= block_threshold:
        email_decision = "BLOCK"
    elif max_score >= flag_threshold:
        email_decision = "FLAG"
    else:
        email_decision = "ALLOW"

    action_risk = check_action_escalation_risk(
        [s for s in segment_results if s.decision != "ALLOW"]
    )

    return EmailClassificationResult(
        message_id=message_id,
        sender=sender,
        subject=subject,
        segment_results=segment_results,
        email_injection_score=round(max_score, 4),
        email_decision=email_decision,
        action_escalation_risk=action_risk,
        audit_notes=audit_notes,
    )


def print_result(r: EmailClassificationResult) -> None:
    esc = " [ACTION ESCALATION RISK]" if r.action_escalation_risk else ""
    print(f"\n[{r.email_decision}] score={r.email_injection_score:.3f}{esc}")
    print(f"  From   : {r.sender[:60]}")
    print(f"  Subject: {r.subject[:60]}")
    for seg in r.segment_results:

Reply Chain Injection Detector

Filename

reply_chain_injection_detector.py

Description

Specialised detector for reply chain and forwarded email injection. Parses email threads to extract all correspondent segments (most recent reply, each quoted prior message, forwarded content blocks), annotates each with its correspondent identity and chain depth, and applies elevated injection suspicion weighting to content from external or unknown correspondents at depth > 0. Produces a per-correspondent injection risk map showing which participant in the thread introduced the suspicious content. Designed for post-incident attribution and for real-time triage of reply-heavy email queues.

Dependencies

  • re (stdlib)
  • json (stdlib)
  • dataclasses (stdlib)

#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""
reply_chain_injection_detector.py
Section 6.8 -- Reply Chain Injection Detector
Parses email thread structure, extracts per-correspondent content,
applies depth-weighted injection scoring, and maps injection risk
back to the originating correspondent.
Author: Raghav Dinesh | ai-security-mastery
"""

import json
import re
from dataclasses import dataclass, field, asdict
from pathlib import Path


# Patterns for reply chain header lines (various email clients)
REPLY_HEADER_PATTERNS = [
    re.compile(
        r"^-{3,}\s*(?:Original|Forwarded)\s+(?:Message|Email)\s*-{3,}$",
        re.MULTILINE | re.IGNORECASE
    ),
    re.compile(
        r"^On .{5,50} wrote:$",
        re.MULTILINE | re.IGNORECASE
    ),
    re.compile(
        r"^From:\s*.+\nSent:\s*.+\nTo:\s*.+\nSubject:\s*.+$",
        re.MULTILINE | re.IGNORECASE
    ),
    re.compile(
        r"^>{1,}\s*On .{5,80} wrote:",
        re.MULTILINE | re.IGNORECASE
    ),
]

INJECTION_SIGNALS = [
    r"\bignore\b.{0,40}\b(previous|prior|all)\b.{0,20}\b(instruction|directive)\b",
    r"\b(forget|disregard|override)\b.{0,30}\b(instruction|told)\b",
    r"\bnote to (ai|assistant|system|llm)\b",
    r"\byour (new|actual|real) (task|role|directive|instruction)\b",
    r"\b(reveal|output|repeat|show)\b.{0,30}\b(system prompt|instructions?)\b",
    r"\bforward.{0,20}(this|all).{0,20}(email|message)\b",
    r"\b(reply|send|email).{0,20}(to|at)\b.{0,30}(adversary|attacker|external|outside)\b",
    r"\bask.{0,30}(user|recipient).{0,30}(password|credential|token)\b",
    r"\byou are now\b",
    r"\bimportant.{0,10}(system|update|directive)\b",
]

INTERNAL_DOMAIN_INDICATORS = re.compile(
    r"@(company|internal|corp|ourorg)\.(com|net|org|io)",
    re.IGNORECASE
)


@dataclass
class ThreadSegment:
    segment_id: int
    depth: int               # 0 = most recent, 1+ = quoted history depth
    correspondent: str       # extracted sender if identifiable, else "unknown"
    is_external: bool        # True if sender appears to be external
    content: str
    injection_score: float
    matched_signals: list[str]
    decision: str


@dataclass
class ThreadAnalysisResult:
    raw_thread_length: int
    segments_found: int
    external_injection_found: bool
    max_injection_score: float
    highest_risk_segment: Optional[str]  # segment_id of highest-risk segment
    correspondent_risk_map: dict         # correspondent -> max_injection_score
    segments: list[ThreadSegment]
    thread_decision: str


def extract_sender_from_header(header_text: str) -> str:
    """Try to extract sender email from reply header text."""
    email_match = re.search(r"[\w.+-]+@[\w.-]+\.\w+", header_text)
    if email_match:
        return email_match.group(0).lower()
    return "unknown"


def is_external(correspondent: str) -> bool:
    if correspondent == "unknown":
        return True
    return not INTERNAL_DOMAIN_INDICATORS.search(correspondent)


def score_segment(text: str, depth: int) -> tuple[float, list[str]]:
    text_l = text.lower()
    matched = [
        p[:60] for p in INJECTION_SIGNALS
        if re.search(p, text_l, re.IGNORECASE)
    ]
    base_score = min(1.0, len(matched) * 0.22)
    # Depth penalty: external quoted content is more suspicious
    depth_weight = 1.0 + min(depth, 3) * 0.2
    return min(1.0, round(base_score * depth_weight, 4)), matched


def split_thread_into_segments(body_text: str) -> list[tuple[int, str, str]]:
    """
    Split email body into segments by reply chain headers.
    Returns list of (depth, correspondent_hint, content) tuples.
    """
    segments = []
    remaining = body_text
    current_depth = 0
    current_correspondent = "current_sender"
    current_content_lines = []

    for line in body_text.splitlines():
        # Check if line is a reply header
        is_header = False
        for pat in REPLY_HEADER_PATTERNS:
            if pat.match(line.strip()):
                # Save current segment
                if current_content_lines:
                    segments.append((
                        current_depth,
                        current_correspondent,
                        "\n".join(current_content_lines)
                    ))
                # Start new segment at increased depth
                current_depth += 1
                current_correspondent = extract_sender_from_header(line)
                current_content_lines = []
                is_header = True
                break

        if not is_header:
            # Count leading > for quote depth
            stripped = line.lstrip("> ")
            gt_count = (len(line) - len(line.lstrip(">")))
            effective_depth = current_depth + gt_count
            current_content_lines.append(stripped)

    # Final segment
    if current_content_lines:
        segments.append((
            current_depth,
            current_correspondent,
            "\n".join(current_content_lines)
        ))

    return segments


def analyse_thread(
    body_text: str,
    flag_threshold: float = 0.30,
    block_threshold: float = 0.55,
) -> ThreadAnalysisResult:
    raw_segments = split_thread_into_segments(body_text)
    thread_segments = []
    correspondent_risk: dict = {}

    for i, (depth, correspondent, content) in enumerate(raw_segments):
        external = is_external(correspondent)
        score, matched = score_segment(content, depth if external else 0)

        if score >= block_threshold:
            decision = "BLOCK"
        elif score >= flag_threshold:
            decision = "FLAG"
        else:
            decision = "ALLOW"

        ts = ThreadSegment(
            segment_id=i,
            depth=depth,
            correspondent=correspondent,
            is_external=external,
            content=content[:120],
            injection_score=score,
            matched_signals=matched,
            decision=decision,
        )
        thread_segments.append(ts)

        # Track per-correspondent risk
        curr_risk = correspondent_risk.get(correspondent, 0.0)
        correspondent_risk[correspondent] = max(curr_risk, score)

    max_score = max((s.injection_score for s in thread_segments), default=0.0)
    highest_risk_seg = max(
        thread_segments, key=lambda s: s.injection_score, default=None
    )

    external_injection = any(
        s.is_external and s.decision != "ALLOW"
        for s in thread_segments
    )

    if max_score >= block_threshold:
        thread_decision = "BLOCK"
    elif max_score >= flag_threshold:
        thread_decision = "FLAG"
    else:
        thread_decision = "ALLOW"

    return ThreadAnalysisResult(
        raw_thread_length=len(body_text),
        segments_found=len(thread_segments),
        external_injection_found=external_injection,
        max_injection_score=round(max_score, 4),
        highest_risk_segment=str(highest_risk_seg.segment_id) if highest_risk_seg else None,
        correspondent_risk_map={k: round(v, 4) for k, v in correspondent_risk.items()},
        segments=thread_segments,
        thread_decision=thread_decision,
    )


def print_analysis(result: ThreadAnalysisResult) -> None:
    ext_flag = " [EXTERNAL INJECTION]" if result.external_injection_found else ""
    print(f"\n[{result.thread_decision}] max_score={result.max_injection_score:.3f}"
          f" segments={result.segments_found}{ext_flag}")
    print("  Correspondent risk map:")
    for corr, risk in result.correspondent_risk_map.items():
        print(f"    {corr:<40} risk={risk:.3f}")
    print("  Segment breakdown:")
    for seg in result.segments:
        ext = " [EXTERNAL]" if seg.is_external else ""
        print(f"    [{seg.decision}] depth={seg.depth} from={seg.correspondent}{ext} "
              f"score={seg.injection_score:.3f}")
        if seg.matched_signals:
            print(f"           match: {seg.matched_signals[0]}")

#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""
email_agent_sandbox_validator.py
Section 6.8 -- Email Agent Sandboxing Checklist Validator
Validates email agent deployment against action restriction and sandboxing
requirements. Produces deployment readiness report.
Author: Raghav Dinesh | ai-security-mastery
"""

import json
from dataclasses import dataclass, field, asdict
from pathlib import Path


@dataclass
class CapabilityControl:
    control_id: str
    capability: str
    description: str
    safe_configuration: str
    deployed_configuration: str
    compliant: bool
    attack_scenario: str
    risk_level: str   # CRITICAL | HIGH | MEDIUM | LOW


@dataclass
class EmailAgentConfig:
    deployment_name: str
    # Outbound email
    outbound_email_enabled: bool = False
    draft_and_hold_enforced: bool = False
    auto_send_to_approved_list_only: bool = False
    human_review_required_before_send: bool = False
    # Calendar
    calendar_integration_enabled: bool = False
    calendar_human_confirm_before_external_publish: bool = False
    # CRM / data integration
    crm_write_enabled: bool = False
    crm_write_staged_review: bool = False
    # Forwarding
    auto_forward_enabled: bool = False
    forward_requires_explicit_authorisation: bool = False
    # Content classification
    pre_processing_classifier_enabled: bool = False
    quoted_content_classified: bool = False
    html_css_stripping_enabled: bool = False
    # Output monitoring
    output_monitoring_enabled: bool = False


def evaluate_config(config: EmailAgentConfig) -> list[CapabilityControl]:
    controls = []

    # Control 1: Outbound email sandboxing
    if config.outbound_email_enabled:
        compliant = (
            config.draft_and_hold_enforced
            and config.human_review_required_before_send
        )
        controls.append(CapabilityControl(
            control_id="EA01",
            capability="outbound_email",
            description="LLM email assistant can compose and send outbound emails",
            safe_configuration=(
                "Draft-and-hold: assistant prepares draft, human reviews "
                "and approves before sending. Auto-send disabled."
            ),
            deployed_configuration=(
                f"draft_and_hold={config.draft_and_hold_enforced}, "
                f"human_review={config.human_review_required_before_send}"
            ),
            compliant=compliant,
            attack_scenario=(
                "Injection in incoming email causes assistant to reply "
                "with system prompt contents or sensitive context. "
                "Without draft-and-hold, the reply is sent immediately "
                "and data is exfiltrated before human review."
            ),
            risk_level="CRITICAL",
        ))

    # Control 2: Calendar integration
    if config.calendar_integration_enabled:
        compliant = config.calendar_human_confirm_before_external_publish
        controls.append(CapabilityControl(
            control_id="EA02",
            capability="calendar_integration",
            description="LLM assistant can create or modify calendar events",
            safe_configuration=(
                "Human confirmation required before publishing events "
                "to external attendees."
            ),
            deployed_configuration=(
                f"human_confirm_before_external_publish="
                f"{config.calendar_human_confirm_before_external_publish}"
            ),
            compliant=compliant,
            attack_scenario=(
                "Injection in calendar invite description causes assistant "
                "to create a follow-up event with adversary-specified "
                "content or attendees."
            ),
            risk_level="HIGH",
        ))

    # Control 3: CRM write
    if config.crm_write_enabled:
        compliant = config.crm_write_staged_review
        controls.append(CapabilityControl(
            control_id="EA03",
            capability="crm_write",
            description="LLM assistant can write records to CRM system",
            safe_configuration=(
                "CRM writes staged in review queue; human commits "
                "before record is persisted."
            ),
            deployed_configuration=(
                f"crm_write_staged_review={config.crm_write_staged_review}"
            ),
            compliant=compliant,
            attack_scenario=(
                "Injection causes assistant to write adversary-controlled "
                "content to CRM record, creating second-order injection "
                "that activates when a subsequent LLM retrieves the record."
            ),
            risk_level="HIGH",
        ))

    # Control 4: Auto-forward restriction
    if config.auto_forward_enabled:
        compliant = config.forward_requires_explicit_authorisation
        controls.append(CapabilityControl(
            control_id="EA04",
            capability="auto_forward",
            description="LLM assistant can forward emails automatically",
            safe_configuration=(
                "Each forward requires explicit per-message authorisation. "
                "No blanket auto-forward rules."
            ),
            deployed_configuration=(
                f"forward_requires_explicit_authorisation="
                f"{config.forward_requires_explicit_authorisation}"
            ),
            compliant=compliant,
            attack_scenario=(
                "Injection causes assistant to forward all incoming emails "
                "to an adversary-controlled address, achieving persistent "
                "email interception."
            ),
            risk_level="CRITICAL",
        ))

    # Control 5: Content classification
    classifier_compliant = (
        config.pre_processing_classifier_enabled
        and config.quoted_content_classified
        and config.html_css_stripping_enabled
    )
    controls.append(CapabilityControl(
        control_id="EA05",
        capability="content_classification",
        description="Email content classified before LLM context delivery",
        safe_configuration=(
            "Pre-processing classifier enabled on all content segments. "
            "Quoted chain content classified independently. "
            "HTML CSS stripping applied before classification."
        ),
        deployed_configuration=(
            f"classifier={config.pre_processing_classifier_enabled}, "
            f"quoted_classified={config.quoted_content_classified}, "
            f"css_stripping={config.html_css_stripping_enabled}"
        ),
        compliant=classifier_compliant,
        attack_scenario=(
            "Without pre-processing classification, injection payloads "
            "in HTML hidden content or quoted thread history enter the "
            "LLM context undetected."
        ),
        risk_level="CRITICAL",
    ))

    return controls


def build_report(
    config: EmailAgentConfig,
    controls: list[CapabilityControl],
) -> str:
    compliant = sum(1 for c in controls if c.compliant)
    non_compliant = [c for c in controls if not c.compliant]
    critical_gaps = [c for c in non_compliant if c.risk_level == "CRITICAL"]

    overall = (
        "DEPLOYMENT_READY" if not non_compliant
        else "NOT_READY" if critical_gaps
        else "CONDITIONAL"
    )

    lines = [
        f"# Email Agent Sandboxing Compliance Report",
        f"**Deployment**: {config.deployment_name}  ",
        f"**Overall status**: {overall}  ",
        f"**Compliant controls**: {compliant}/{len(controls)}  ",
        "",
        "---",
        "",
        "## Controls Summary",
        "",
        "| ID | Capability | Risk | Status |",
        "|----|-----------|------|--------|",
    ]
    for c in controls:
        status = "COMPLIANT" if c.compliant else f"NON_COMPLIANT ({c.risk_level})"
        lines.append(f"| {c.control_id} | {c.capability} | {c.risk_level} | {status} |")

    if non_compliant:
        lines += ["", "---", "", "## Remediation Required", ""]
        for c in sorted(non_compliant, key=lambda x: 0 if x.risk_level == "CRITICAL" else 1):
            lines += [
                f"### [{c.risk_level}] {c.control_id}: {c.capability}",
                "",
                f"Current: {c.deployed_configuration}",
                f"Required: {c.safe_configuration}",
                f"Attack scenario: {c.attack_scenario}",
                "",
            ]
    return "\n".join(lines)


def run_demo() -> None:
    config = EmailAgentConfig(
        deployment_name="Enterprise Email Assistant v2",
        outbound_email_enabled=True,
        draft_and_hold_enforced=True,
        auto_send_to_approved_list_only=False,
        human_review_required_before_send=True,
        calendar_integration_enabled=True,
        calendar_human_confirm_before_external_publish=False,  # gap
        crm_write_enabled=True,
        crm_write_staged_review=False,                         # gap
        auto_forward_enabled=False,
        forward_requires_explicit_authorisation=True,
        pre_processing_classifier_enabled=True,
        quoted_content_classified=True,
        html_css_stripping_enabled=False,                      # gap
        output_monitoring_enabled=True,
    )

    controls = evaluate_config(config)
    report_md = build_report(config, controls)

    non_compliant = [c for c in controls if not c.compliant]
    print(f"[*] {config.deployment_name}")
    print(f"[*] Compliant: {len(controls) - len(non_compliant)}/{len(controls)}")
    for c in non_compliant:
        print(f"    [{c.risk_level}] {c.control_id}: {c.capability}")

    Path("email_sandbox_report.md").write_text(report_md, encoding="utf-8")
    Path("email_sandbox_results.json").write_text(
        json.dumps({"controls": [asdict(c) for c in controls]}, indent=2),
        encoding="utf-8"
    )
    print("[OK] Report written to email_sandbox_report.md")


if __name__ == "__main__":
    run_demo()

Section Summary

Section 6.8 established email as the most accessible indirect injection surface. The adversary delivers the attack vector directly to the target organisation via email -- no access to knowledge bases, web infrastructure, or target systems required. Plain-text body injection, subject line injection, HTML CSS-hidden injection, reply chain injection, forwarded email injection, and calendar invite field injection are six distinct entry points, each with different adversary access requirements and detection characteristics. LLM email assistants with send capability are the highest-impact deployment scenario: an injection that causes the assistant to reply, forward, or take external action achieves adversary objectives using the assistant's legitimate permissions. The confused deputy attack is direct and immediate. Draft-and-hold -- requiring human review before any outbound email is sent -- is the single most important sandboxing control for email agent deployments. Reply chain and forwarded email injection bypass trusted sender heuristics by embedding payloads in quoted content attributed to external correspondents. Per-segment depth-weighted classification is the detection approach: each quoted segment is classified independently, with depth and externality increasing the injection suspicion weight. Section 6.9 moves to the database injection surface -- where adversaries plant payloads in structured data fields that LLMs query and use as context, creating second-order injection patterns that are the most persistent of the indirect injection classes.
